A mixie is exactly what the name suggests: a mix of pixie and mullet. Through the front, top and sides it is cut short and neat like a classic pixie, but instead of tapering off at the nape, the back keeps real length — textured, piecey strands that fall over the back of the neck. From the front it reads polished and deliberate. From behind, it has edge.

That front-to-back split is why the cut has taken off. A full mullet can feel like a big commitment, and a full pixie can feel like a bigger one. The mixie sits between the two: you get the face-framing shortness and low bulk of a pixie, with a softer, more forgiving silhouette at the back. It also grows out more gracefully than either parent cut, which matters more than most people expect when they are choosing short hair.

## 1. What Defines a Mixie

The defining feature is the contrast. The top and sides are pixie-short — cropped close around the ears and through the crown — while the back section is left noticeably longer, usually reaching somewhere between the nape and the collar. If the back is only slightly longer than a standard pixie taper, it is still a pixie. Once the back length is long enough to move, flick out and be styled as its own section, you are in mixie territory.

The second feature is texture. A mixie is almost never blunt. Stylists cut it with razors or point-cutting to keep the back length wispy and separated rather than heavy, because a solid block of length at the back tips the whole cut into mullet cosplay. The front stays cleaner — a soft fringe, a side sweep or short textured layers — so the face still looks framed rather than overgrown.

## 2. Eleven Mixie Styles to Show Your Stylist

### Classic Mixie

The reference version: short, softly layered top, tidy sides that clear the ears, and two to three inches of textured length at the back. Nothing is exaggerated, which makes it the easiest entry point if you are coming from a bob or a longer pixie.

Wear it tucked behind the ears for a cleaner finish, or push the top forward for more texture. It suits almost every hair type because the proportions stay balanced.

### Soft Feminine Mixie

This version keeps the back length but softens everything around it — longer, feathery layers on top, a wispy fringe, and sides that skim the cheekbone instead of clearing the ear completely. The mullet element is there, just quieter.

It is a good choice if you like the idea of the cut but want it to read more romantic than edgy. A little shine cream through the back keeps the length looking intentional rather than accidental.

### Shaggy Mixie

The shaggy mixie leans into the mullet side: choppier layers, more separation, and a back section cut with a razor so the ends flick and piece out. The top is messier too, with short, uneven layers throughout.

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/mixie-haircut/mixie-haircut-02.webp" alt="Shaggy mixie haircut with choppy razor-cut layers and flicked-out ends at the back" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

This is the lowest-effort version to style because it is supposed to look undone. Scrunch in a texture spray, push it around with your fingers, and let it dry however it wants.

### Mixie with Curtain Fringe

Here the short top is paired with a centre-parted curtain fringe that opens at the brow and blends into the sides. The fringe softens the contrast between the short front and long back, so the whole cut flows more.

It works particularly well on oval and longer face shapes, where the curtain pieces add width at the cheekbones. Expect the fringe to need a quick pass with a round brush or straightener most mornings.

### Mixie with Micro Fringe

The opposite approach: a short, blunt micro fringe that makes the pixie front look sharper and more deliberate. Against the soft length at the back, the contrast is the whole point.

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/mixie-haircut/mixie-haircut-03.webp" alt="Mixie haircut with a short blunt micro fringe and longer piecey length at the back" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Micro fringes are honest haircuts — they show every millimetre of growth, so plan on fringe trims between appointments. On the plus side, they grow into a soft full fringe within a few weeks if you change your mind.

### Curly Mixie

Curls change the proportions of a mixie in a good way. The back length springs up, so a curly mixie usually needs more length left at the back than a straight one to read the same — something to discuss with your stylist before the scissors come out.

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/mixie-haircut/mixie-haircut-04.webp" alt="Curly mixie haircut with short curly layers on top and springy curls falling at the back of the neck" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Cut dry, curl by curl, the shape keeps the volume on top where you want it and lets the back curls do their own thing. If you already wear [curly pixie cuts](/hairstyles/bangs-bobs/curly-pixie-cuts/), this is the natural next step when you want more length without growing everything out.

### Mixie with Undercut Sides

For maximum contrast, the sides are clipped short — sometimes down to a true undercut — while the top and back keep their length. It is the boldest version, and the one that grows out most noticeably at the sides.

If you like the shaved-side drama of [undercut pixie cuts](/hairstyles/bangs-bobs/undercut-pixie-cuts/) but found the all-over shortness too much, this splits the difference. The back length keeps it from reading severe.

### Long Mixie

The long mixie pushes the front longer too — the top and sides reach jaw length, close to a bob in front — while the back extends further down the neck. From the front, people may not even realise it is a mixie until you turn around.

This is the version closest to [pixie bob haircuts](/hairstyles/bangs-bobs/pixie-bob-haircuts/), and it is the easiest to grow out into a full bob later, because the front is already most of the way there.

### Mixie for Fine Hair

Fine hair can wear a mixie, but the proportions need adjusting. Too much back length on fine hair goes wispy and see-through quickly, so the back is kept shorter and the layers blunter to hold what density there is.

Styled with a volumising mousse at the roots and the back pieces defined with a dab of wax, the wispiness becomes a feature — soft, feathery movement rather than a solid curtain of hair. It will not look like the thick-haired reference photos, and a good stylist will say so upfront.

### Grown-Out Mixie

Every mixie becomes this eventually: the top has lengthened, the sides have softened, and the back has grown past the collar. Styled deliberately — tucked, textured, with the fringe swept to one side — it reads as an intentional shaggy short cut rather than a haircut you missed.

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/mixie-haircut/mixie-haircut-05.webp" alt="Grown-out mixie haircut with longer softened layers styled with a side-swept fringe" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Many people actually prefer their mixie at this stage. If that turns out to be you, tell your stylist — they can start cutting it as a grown-out shape from the beginning and save you the in-between awkwardness.

### Platinum and Coloured Mixie

Short on top, long at the back, and two textures to play with — the mixie is built for colour. Platinum all over is the statement version; softer options include a brighter panel through the fringe or darker roots that melt into lighter back lengths.

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/mixie-haircut/mixie-haircut-06.webp" alt="Platinum blonde mixie haircut with a short bright top and longer icy length at the back" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Because the cut gets trimmed often, it is also forgiving for colour experiments: bleach-damaged ends at the back are regularly cut away rather than nursed along for months.

## Mixie vs Bixie vs Wolf Cut vs Pixie

These four get confused constantly, so here is the honest split. A pixie is short all over, including the back — no length at the nape. A mixie is a pixie in front with deliberate mullet length behind. A [bixie](/hairstyles/bangs-bobs/bixie-cuts/) is a bob-pixie blend: longer and heavier than a pixie all over, with a bob's outline, and crucially no mullet back — the length is even rather than concentrated behind. A [wolf cut](/hairstyles/haircuts/wolf-cut-for-women/) is a longer shag-mullet hybrid worn at shoulder length and beyond, with heavy layers throughout; think of it as the mixie's bigger, longer cousin rather than a short cut at all.

If you are torn between short options, [long pixie cuts](/hairstyles/bangs-bobs/long-pixie-cuts/) are worth a look too — they keep the back neat but give you more length on top to play with.

## Who It Suits

Oval and heart-shaped faces take to the mixie most easily, since the short top and cheekbone-level sides need little correction. On square faces, the soft feminine or curtain-fringe versions work better than the undercut, because the wispy back length softens the jaw. Round faces do well with height on top and the longer back, which visually lengthens the silhouette — just keep the sides close rather than bulky.

For texture, straight to wavy hair shows the pixie-mullet split most clearly. Curls work beautifully with adjusted proportions. Very fine hair is the one honest caveat: the back length will look wispy rather than full. That can absolutely be a feature — a soft, feathery finish — but if you want the dense, piecey back from most reference photos, fine hair will not deliver it, and a shorter-backed mixie or a bixie may make you happier.

## Styling and the Grow-Out

Day to day, a mixie is a five-minute haircut. The top wants a pea-sized amount of matte paste or texture cream worked through with fingers; the back wants defining, not flattening — scrunch the ends so they piece out rather than lying as one flat sheet. Most versions air-dry well, which is part of the appeal.

The honest maintenance note: the back length is the first thing to look untidy. The top can grow for weeks and still read as a style, but the back crosses from "textured" to "straggly" faster, especially on straighter hair. Most wearers settle into a tidy-up every four to six weeks, with the back and fringe trimmed between full cuts if needed. The good news is the grow-out path is kind — a mixie grows into a shaggy bob shape rather than the helmet stage a classic pixie goes through, so if you decide to grow it out, you are looking at months of wearable in-between stages rather than one long awkward one.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What's the difference between a mixie and a bixie?
A mixie keeps a short pixie shape at the front with longer mullet length concentrated at the back. A bixie blends a pixie with a bob, so it is longer and more even all over, with no mullet back. If the extra length sits behind your neck, it is a mixie; if it sits around your jaw, it is a bixie.

### Does a mixie suit fine hair?
Yes, with adjusted proportions. Fine hair needs the back length kept shorter and the layers cut blunter so the ends do not go see-through. The finish will look softer and wispier than it does on thick hair, which many people like, but it will not match the dense, piecey look in most reference photos.

### How long does a mixie take to grow out?
A mixie grows out more gracefully than a classic pixie because the back length is already there. Most people can grow it into a shaggy bob within about four to six months of trims that only clean up the shape. Growing it to a full one-length bob takes longer, usually the better part of a year.

### Is a mixie hard to style every day?
No, it is one of the easier short cuts to live with. Most versions need only a small amount of texture product worked through with your fingers, and they air-dry well. Fringe versions take a minute or two more, since a micro or curtain fringe usually wants a quick brush-through or heat pass to sit properly.

### Can you get a mixie with curly hair?
Yes, and curls suit the shape well. Because curls spring up, your stylist should leave more length at the back than they would on straight hair to achieve the same look. Ask for the cut to be done dry, curl by curl, so the proportions are judged on your hair as you actually wear it.

### How often does a mixie need a trim?
Most mixies want a tidy-up every four to six weeks. The back length starts looking untidy before the top does, so some people book a quick back and fringe trim between full appointments. If you prefer the grown-out version of the cut, you can stretch appointments further and just keep the ends clean.
