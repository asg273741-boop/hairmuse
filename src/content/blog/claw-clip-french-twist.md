A claw clip can make a French twist feel less formal and easier to adjust during the day. The key is to give the clip something substantial to grip: the folded seam of the twist, rather than a thin layer of hair on the surface.

This version works best on hair that reaches at least the shoulders. If your hair is much longer or thicker, use a clip with deep teeth and a strong spring. Prefer pins? Our [soft bobby pin updos](/hairstyles/updos-buns/soft-bobby-pin-updos/) guide builds the same folded seam with bobby pins instead.

## What you need

- A claw clip large enough to span the width of your twist
- A brush or wide-tooth comb
- Optional: dry texture spray for very slippery hair, or a few bobby pins for shorter layers

As a rough guide, fine or shoulder-length hair usually suits a medium clip around 7–8 cm long, while thick or long hair needs a large clip of 10 cm or more. The teeth should close over the whole twist with a little room to spare — a clip that only just closes will pop open or slide within the hour.

## Build the twist

1. **Gather hair low.** Brush the hair back with your hands, leaving the front as smooth or loose as you prefer. Hold the length at the nape as though you were about to make a low ponytail. Gather it a fraction off-centre, on the side you find most comfortable; the twist will roll inward and finish in the middle.
2. **Turn the length upward.** Twist the ponytail toward the crown two or three times. Keep one hand at the base so the twist stays close to your head. Always twist in the same direction, rolling the hair inward toward the centre back; twisting outward makes the seam unravel as soon as you let go. It should feel snug but never tight enough to pull at the hairline.
3. **Fold in the ends.** Bring the free ends back down alongside the twist and tuck them underneath it. Long hair may need a second fold; keep the bulk of the fold in the centre rather than piling it at the top. Aim for a flat pocket of folded hair running down the seam, with the ends lying flat along the twist rather than sticking out.
4. **Place the clip across the seam.** Open the clip wide. Set one side of its teeth into the folded hair and the other against the hair close to your scalp. Close it over the centre of the twist. The clip sits horizontally or very slightly diagonal, its teeth passing through the fold to catch the layer beneath; if they only skim the surface, push the clip a few millimetres deeper.
5. **Check the tension.** Gently move your head. If the style pulls at the hairline, loosen the twist slightly and reclip it lower. If it slides, move the clip deeper into the folded seam.

You can leave a few front pieces loose or smooth them back. Neither changes the structure of the twist.

## Make it hold all day

Freshly washed, silky hair is the hardest to keep in place, so start with grip rather than trying to rescue a sliding style later. Day-two hair holds best; if your hair is clean, mist dry shampoo or texture spray through the mid-lengths and ends *before* you gather it.

For extra security, anchor the base first: cross two bobby pins in an X at the nape, then build the twist over them. The pins stop the whole fold travelling downward — the same grip principles behind our guide to [messy buns that hold](/hairstyles/updos-buns/messy-buns-that-hold/): texture first, then an anchor, then the visible fastening.

Very thick hair often does better with two clips than one oversized one. Place the first across the middle of the seam as above, then add a second, smaller clip just above or below it so the fold is held at two points and cannot rotate loose.

## Adjust it for your hair

**Fine or freshly washed hair:** A small amount of texture spray through the mid-lengths can add grip. Start with less than you think you need; too much product can make the fold stiff and difficult to reposition. A smaller, lighter clip also helps — a heavy clip in fine hair drags the fold down by its own weight.

**Thick hair:** Choose a clip with a wider opening and longer teeth. If one clip cannot close without strain, use two medium clips placed vertically along the seam.

**Short layers:** Tuck escaping pieces toward the centre and secure them with a bobby pin before clipping. A little movement around the face is fine; only pin the pieces that keep pulling the twist apart.

## Variations to try

**Low twist.** Gather the hair at the very bottom of the nape and keep the fold shorter, so the twist sits like a neat roll just above the neckline. This is the softest version and the most forgiving on shorter hair.

**Side twist.** Gather the hair over one shoulder, twist upward on that side and clip the seam diagonally. The off-centre finish suits anyone who finds a centred seam shows the clip too clearly.

**Half-up twist.** Take only the top half of the hair, twist and clip it at the back of the crown, and leave the rest loose. It gives the same polished front view with none of the tension, and makes a good practice version while you learn the fold. ## If the clip keeps sliding

Take the style down and rebuild it with a firmer fold at the nape. Clipping only the outer layer is the usual cause of a slipping twist. The clip should hold both the fold and some hair near the scalp. If it feels uncomfortable, change the clip size or placement rather than tightening further.

**Ends poking out:** The fold is too shallow or the ends are too long for a single fold. Fold the ends twice so the tips end up buried in the middle of the pocket, or pin the tip flat against the seam with one bobby pin before clipping over it.

**The clip is too visible:** Choose a clip close to your hair colour, angle the seam slightly so the clip sits flatter, or pull one loose strand from above the clip and let it fall over the top edge. A matte finish also reads as far less obvious than a glossy one.

**It loosens by midday:** Hair relaxes as the fold settles. Rather than adding more pins, take the clip out, give the twist one extra turn and reclip — twenty seconds, and the tension is properly reset.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What size claw clip do I need for a French twist?

Match the clip to the amount of hair the fold contains, not just to your hair length. Fine or shoulder-length hair usually works with a medium clip around 7–8 cm, while thick or long hair needs a large clip of 10 cm or more with long, widely spaced teeth. The teeth should close over the whole seam without straining; a clip you have to force shut is too small and will pop open or slide out.

### How do I make a claw clip twist hold in thick hair?

Give the clip two chances to grip instead of one. Build the twist with a firm fold, anchor the base with two crossed bobby pins, then use either one extra-large clip with deep teeth or two medium clips placed along the seam. Splitting the fold into two clipped stages — lower half first, then the rest over the top — also stops the hair's weight rotating a single clip loose.

### My hair is fine and slippery. Will a claw clip twist stay in?

Yes, with texture added first. Mist dry shampoo or texture spray through the mid-lengths before you start, or style the twist on day-two hair when natural oils give the teeth something to hold. Lightly backcombing the inside of the fold helps too. Choose a small, lightweight clip — a heavy clip pulls a fine-hair twist down by its own weight — and expect to reclip once during the day; that is normal, not a failure.

### Is a claw clip French twist smart enough for work or a formal event?

It can be. The shape is the same classic silhouette as a pinned twist, so with a smooth finish, tucked ends and a plain clip near your hair colour, it reads as polished rather than casual. For weddings and formal occasions, a matte or tortoiseshell clip looks more deliberate than a glossy plastic one. It is less suitable only where you need the style to survive dancing or weather completely untouched.

### Will a claw clip damage my hair, and how do I take the twist down?

A claw clip is one of the gentler fastenings, because it spreads pressure across many teeth instead of concentrating it in one elastic band. Damage usually comes from forcing an undersized clip shut or ripping it out. To remove the twist, open the clip fully and lift it straight out rather than dragging it through the fold, then unwind the hair with your fingers. Avoid sleeping in the clip, as the teeth can press into the scalp and snap strands while you move.

### How is a claw clip twist different from a classic French twist with pins?

The shaping is identical — gather, twist upward, fold the ends into a seam — but the fastening and feel differ. A pinned twist hides its structure, sits flatter and holds longer, which is why it suits formal events; a claw clip version takes under a minute, can be redone during the day, and leaves no pin marks. Most people learn the shape faster with a clip, then move to pins once the fold feels automatic.
