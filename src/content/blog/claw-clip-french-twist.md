A claw clip can make a French twist feel less formal and easier to adjust during the day. The key is to give the clip something substantial to grip: the folded seam of the twist, rather than a thin layer of hair on the surface.

This version works best on hair that reaches at least the shoulders. If your hair is much longer or thicker, use a clip with deep teeth and a strong spring.

## What you need

- A claw clip large enough to span the width of your twist
- A brush or wide-tooth comb
- Optional: dry texture spray for very slippery hair, or a few bobby pins for shorter layers

## Build the twist

1. **Gather hair low.** Brush the hair back with your hands, leaving the front as smooth or loose as you prefer. Hold the length at the nape as though you were about to make a low ponytail.
2. **Turn the length upward.** Twist the ponytail toward the crown two or three times. Keep one hand at the base so the twist stays close to your head.
3. **Fold in the ends.** Bring the free ends back down alongside the twist and tuck them underneath it. Long hair may need a second fold; keep the bulk of the fold in the center rather than piling it at the top.
4. **Place the clip across the seam.** Open the clip wide. Set one side of its teeth into the folded hair and the other against the hair close to your scalp. Close it over the center of the twist.
5. **Check the tension.** Gently move your head. If the style pulls at the hairline, loosen the twist slightly and reclip it lower. If it slides, move the clip deeper into the folded seam.

You can leave a few front pieces loose or smooth them back. Neither changes the structure of the twist.

## Adjust it for your hair

**Fine or freshly washed hair:** A small amount of texture spray through the mid-lengths can add grip. Start with less than you think you need; too much product can make the fold stiff and difficult to reposition.

**Thick hair:** Choose a clip with a wider opening and longer teeth. If one clip cannot close without strain, use two medium clips placed vertically along the seam.

**Short layers:** Tuck escaping pieces toward the center and secure them with a bobby pin before clipping. A little movement around the face is fine; only pin the pieces that keep pulling the twist apart.

## If the clip keeps sliding

Take the style down and rebuild it with a firmer fold at the nape. Clipping only the outer layer is the usual cause of a slipping twist. The clip should hold both the fold and some hair near the scalp. If it feels uncomfortable, change the clip size or placement rather than tightening the hair further.
