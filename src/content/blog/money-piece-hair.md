A money piece is the hardest-working two sections of hair in the salon: the strands either side of your parting, lightened brighter than everything behind them so your face gets its own lighting. It is a small area of colour with an outsized effect — it brightens your complexion, makes the base colour behind it look richer, and photographs like a ring light you wear. It is also one of the most forgiving ways into colour, because so little hair is lightened.

The twelve looks below cover every base colour, from the classic blonde-on-brunette to a regrowth-friendly whisper you could wear to the strictest office. If your base sits in the brown family, our [light brown hair colour ideas](/hairstyles/hair-color/light-brown-hair-color-ideas/) pair naturally with several of these placements.

---

## What Is a Money Piece, Exactly?

A money piece is a placement, not a technique: two face-framing sections at the front hairline, coloured noticeably lighter than the rest of your hair. It differs from ordinary highlights, which are distributed evenly through the whole head — a money piece concentrates nearly all the brightness at the front, where it does the most for your face. It differs from balayage in focus rather than method: balayage is hand-painted lightness through the mid-lengths and ends all over, while a money piece is specifically the front framing — though the two combine beautifully, as look 8 shows. Think of highlights as overall lighting and a money piece as a spotlight.

## 1. Classic Blonde Money Piece on Brunette

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/money-piece-hair/money-piece-hair-01.webp" alt="Woman with dark brunette hair and bright blonde money piece highlights framing her face" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

The original: dark brunette hair with two bright, clean blonde panels at the front. The contrast is the point — the blonde makes the brunette look deeper and the brunette makes the blonde look brighter, and your face sits in the glow between them. It suits oval and heart face shapes particularly well, and warm-to-neutral undertones that can carry a golden-leaning blonde. Upkeep is moderate: the front sections show regrowth first, so plan a front-only refresh every six to eight weeks. Ask your stylist for a bold money piece two to three levels lighter than your base, blended at the root so it grows out softly rather than in a stripe.

## 2. Caramel Money Piece on Dark Brown

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/money-piece-hair/money-piece-hair-02.webp" alt="Woman with dark brown hair and warm caramel money piece strands around her face" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Caramel softens the classic into something warmer and more wearable: toffee-toned front sections on a dark brown base, only a level or two of contrast. It is the kindest version for olive and warm undertones, and it flatters round faces because the warmth sits high and draws the eye upward. Upkeep is low — caramel fades gracefully and the softer contrast hides regrowth for eight to ten weeks. Ask for a warm caramel money piece with a root smudge, the exact approach that makes our [brunette balayage](/hairstyles/hair-color/brunette-balayage/) so low-maintenance through the lengths.

## 3. Copper Money Piece

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/money-piece-hair/money-piece-hair-03.webp" alt="Woman with auburn brown hair and vivid copper money piece highlights at the front" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Copper front sections on a brown or auburn base give the money piece a fiery edge — the face frame glows russet in daylight and makes green and hazel eyes look almost lit from inside. It suits warm undertones and freckled skin best, and it is a natural partner if you already love the tones in our [cherry cola brunette guide](/hairstyles/hair-color/cherry-cola-brunette-hair/). Be honest about upkeep: red pigment fades fastest of all, so budget for a copper gloss or colour-depositing conditioner every few weeks. Ask your stylist for a true copper, not a strawberry blonde — the depth is what keeps it looking expensive.

## 4. Platinum Money Piece on Black Hair

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/money-piece-hair/money-piece-hair-04.webp" alt="Woman with black hair and striking platinum blonde money piece panels framing her face" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Maximum drama: icy platinum panels against a black base. Nothing on this list turns heads faster, and the graphic contrast suits sharp features, square jawlines and cool undertones that can handle an almost-white blonde. It is also the highest-commitment look here. Reaching platinum from black takes serious lightening — expect a long first appointment, possibly two — and the front sections will need toning every four to six weeks to stay icy rather than yellow. Ask for a strand test first; a good colourist will insist on one before promising platinum, and that caution protects the very hair everyone sees.

## 5. Soft Beige Money Piece on Blonde

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/money-piece-hair/money-piece-hair-05.webp" alt="Woman with blonde hair and soft beige brighter strands framing her face" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Money pieces are not only for dark bases. On an all-over blonde, a slightly brighter, beige-toned front section adds dimension that flat blonde often lacks — the face frame catches light differently from the hair behind it, so the whole colour looks more expensive. It suits cool and neutral undertones and is gentle on fine blonde hair, because the lift needed is minimal. Upkeep is low to moderate: a toner refresh with your usual appointment keeps the beige clean. Ask for brightness just one level above your base, toned beige rather than ash, so the effect reads as light rather than grey.

## 6. Chunky Y2K Contrast Money Piece

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/money-piece-hair/money-piece-hair-06.webp" alt="Woman with dark hair and thick chunky blonde Y2K money piece stripes at the front" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

The deliberate throwback: wide, unblended, high-contrast front stripes that reference early-2000s pop styling and make no apology for it. Where the classic money piece blends at the root, the Y2K version starts bold right at the hairline. It suits confident styling, oval and long face shapes, and anyone whose wardrobe already leans retro. Upkeep is higher than it looks — that crisp root line shows regrowth within four to six weeks, and keeping the stripes chunky rather than stripy takes a colourist who sections generously. Ask for wide panels and minimal blending; subtlety is a different look on this list.

## 7. Subtle Face Frame on Red Hair

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/money-piece-hair/money-piece-hair-07.webp" alt="Woman with red hair and slightly lighter copper-blonde strands framing her face" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Red hair takes a money piece differently: instead of blonde, the front sections go a shade or two lighter within the red family — copper-blonde or bright ginger against a deeper auburn. The effect is sunlight rather than contrast, and it keeps red hair looking natural while still lifting the face. It suits warm and neutral undertones and is lovely on round faces, where the brighter front softens the edges of the face shape. Upkeep mirrors red hair generally — glosses to fight fade, every six to eight weeks. Ask your stylist to stay inside the red family; a true blonde frame on red hair usually reads as a mistake rather than a choice.

## 8. Money Piece with Balayage

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/money-piece-hair/money-piece-hair-08.webp" alt="Woman with brunette balayage and a brighter blonde money piece framing her face" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

The best of both placements: hand-painted balayage dimension through the lengths, with the front sections taken brighter to finish the frame. If you already wear the look in our [brunette balayage guide](/hairstyles/hair-color/brunette-balayage/), this is simply asking for the front to go one step further at your next appointment. It suits every face shape and undertone, because both the balayage tones and the frame brightness are mixed to order. Upkeep is the friendliest of the bold looks — the balayage grows out invisibly and only the front needs a refresh every eight weeks or so. Ask for exactly that split when you book.

## 9. Money Piece on Curly Hair

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/money-piece-hair/money-piece-hair-09.webp" alt="Woman with dark curly hair and lighter golden money piece curls framing her face" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Curls change the geometry: the money piece follows the curls that naturally fall at the front, so the brightness moves and catches light as the hair does. Golden or caramel tones work best on dark curly bases, and the placement flatters round and heart face shapes by putting lightness at the temples and cheekbones. Upkeep is moderate, with one curly-specific caveat — lightened curls are thirstier, so a weekly mask is part of the deal. Ask for a colourist who colours curls dry and in their natural fall; a money piece placed on straightened hair lands in the wrong place once the curls spring back.

## 10. Soft, Regrowth-Friendly Money Piece

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/money-piece-hair/money-piece-hair-10.webp" alt="Woman with medium brown hair and a soft diffused money piece blending gently at the roots" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

The low-commitment version: front sections only a level lighter than your base, feathered and diffused at the root so there is never a line to grow out. It is the money piece for anyone who has been colour-shy, because it reads as naturally sun-lightened hair rather than a salon decision. It suits all face shapes and undertones, and fine or thinning hair at the front benefits most — the soft brightness disguises a sparse hairline better than contrast would. Upkeep is minimal: ten to twelve weeks, or simply let it fade into your base entirely. Ask for a diffused, root-melted money piece one level up.

## 11. Bold Fashion-Colour Money Piece

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/money-piece-hair/money-piece-hair-11.webp" alt="Woman with dark hair and vivid pink money piece panels framing her face" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Swap the blonde for a statement: vivid pink, cobalt, lilac or emerald front panels on a dark base. Because only two sections carry the fashion colour, you get the full effect with a fraction of the bleach, the cost and the commitment of an all-over vivid — and if you tire of it, two sections are easy to recolour or grow out. It suits any face shape; choose the shade by undertone (cool pinks and blues for cool skin, coral and copper-reds for warm). Upkeep is the honest catch: vivids fade within weeks, so plan on a colour-depositing conditioner at home. Ask your stylist to lift the front clean and pale — fashion colour is only ever as vivid as the base beneath it.

## 12. Subtle Money Piece for Fine Hair

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/money-piece-hair/money-piece-hair-12.webp" alt="Woman with fine light brown hair and a delicate subtle money piece adding brightness at the front" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Fine hair needs the lightest touch on this list: narrow, delicately woven front sections just bright enough to suggest dimension. Done well, a subtle money piece makes fine hair look thicker, because the eye reads the brightness variation as density. It suits all undertones — match the tone to your skin as in the section below — and it is the safest first colour for fragile hair, since so little is lightened and the lift is small. Upkeep is low, every eight to ten weeks. Ask for fine weaves rather than panels, and a bond-building treatment in the service; on fine hair, condition is the whole game.

## Choosing Your Brightness: Match It to Your Undertone

The level of contrast is only half the decision; the tone of the front sections matters just as much. Warm undertones glow with golden, caramel and copper money pieces — the warmth in the hair echoes the warmth in the skin. Cool undertones want icy, ash or platinum-leaning brightness, and a golden frame on cool skin can look brassy within weeks. Neutral undertones have the widest choice, with beige and mushroom-leaning blondes as the safest ground. The quick test: if gold jewellery flatters you more than silver, go warm; if silver wins, go cool. When in doubt, ask your colourist to tone the front a fraction cooler than you think — money pieces photograph warmer than they look in the mirror.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does a money piece last?

The colour itself is permanent lightening, so it does not wash out — it grows out. How long it looks fresh depends on contrast: bold versions like looks 1 and 4 show regrowth in six to eight weeks, while soft, diffused versions like look 10 stay presentable for ten to twelve. A front-only refresh is a short, cheaper appointment than a full colour, which is much of the money piece's appeal.

### Does a money piece damage your hair?

Far less than most colour services, simply because so little hair is lightened — two front sections rather than a full head. The exception is high-lift versions on dark bases, like platinum on black, where those front sections take real bleach work. A bond-building treatment during the service and a weekly mask afterwards keep the front sections — the hair everyone sees — in good condition.

### How much does a money piece cost?

Expect it to cost less than a full set of highlights, because it is less hair and less time: many salons price a face-frame service at roughly half to two-thirds of a partial highlight, though vivid and platinum versions cost more for the extra lifting and toning. Prices vary widely by city and salon, so ask for the face-frame price specifically when you book — it is often listed separately.

### How does a money piece grow out?

That depends on the root blend. Versions melted or smudged at the root (looks 2, 8 and 10) soften into your base with no visible line, and can simply be left to fade into a gentle face frame. Bold, root-to-fabric versions like the Y2K stripes grow out with a clear line and need refreshing or re-blending every four to six weeks to keep the intended look.

### Does a money piece suit a round face?

Yes — it is one of the most flattering placements for round faces. Brightness at the front draws the eye vertically and creates the impression of length, especially when the lightest strands sit at the temples and cheekbones rather than at jaw level. Softer contrast (looks 2 and 7) tends to flatter more than hard stripes, which can widen the face visually.

### Can a money piece be subtle enough for work?

Absolutely — looks 10 and 12 were designed for exactly that. A money piece one level lighter than your base, diffused at the root, reads as sun-lightened natural hair rather than a colour service, and it passes in even conservative workplaces. If your office is strict, stay within your own colour family and avoid fashion shades and high-contrast stripes.

## Final Thoughts

A money piece is the smallest colour change with the largest return: two sections of hair, chosen by undertone and placed with intent, doing the work of a full colour service. Start softer than you think if you are new to it — you can always go brighter at the refresh, and the soft versions grow out so kindly that there is nothing to regret. Bring your colourist two photographs, as always: the brightness you want, and the contrast you are afraid of. The space between those two pictures is your money piece.
