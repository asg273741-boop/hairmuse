Mermaid waves are deep, uniform S-waves with a regular, almost crimped rhythm — every ridge sits the same distance from the next, and the whole length has a glossy, polished finish. They are named for the long, flowing result: hair that looks like it is moving in water even when it is perfectly still. Where beach waves are loose, matte and deliberately irregular, mermaid waves are the opposite — structured, shiny and even from root to tip.

Each wave is a broad, flat S rather than a round curl, which is why the style reads as elegant rather than tousled. It works best on medium to very long hair, because the pattern needs length to repeat. This guide covers the triple-barrel method in full, a flat-iron alternative, a heatless braid option, and the fixes for waves that drop or frizz.

## 1. What Defines Mermaid Waves

Uniformity is the whole look. A mermaid wave is not a curl that has been brushed out — it is a pressed wave, formed by clamping hair between shaped barrels so the S-pattern is identical along the entire strand. Three things separate it from every other wave style:

- **Regular spacing.** The ridges repeat at even intervals, like a ribbon folded back and forth. Irregular spacing reads as beachy instead.
- **Flat, broad waves.** The hair lies in wide S-curves rather than spiralling, with no twist in the strand, which is why the finish looks smooth instead of textured.
- **Gloss.** Pressed flat rather than roughed up, the hair reflects light evenly. Matte products and heavy texturisers work against the look.

Length matters more here than in most styles. On very long hair the pattern can repeat six or seven times down the strand, which is where the full effect comes from. On medium hair you get three or four repeats — still clearly mermaid waves. On short hair the pattern barely completes one full S, which is why the bob variation later needs an honest adjustment. The waves also usually start a few centimetres below the root, not at the scalp — that gap of smoother hair at the top keeps the style modern and stops it looking like a full crimp.

## 2. The Triple-Barrel Waver Method, Step by Step

A triple-barrel waver (sometimes called a mermaid waver or deep waver) is the tool this style was built around. Two outer barrels and a centre barrel press the hair into the S-shape in one clamp — faster and far more consistent than any alternative. Work on completely dry hair: a waver on damp hair steams rather than sets, and the waves will not hold.

1. **Prep and protect.** Brush the hair smooth and apply heat protectant evenly through the lengths. If your hair is fine or drops styles easily, work a light mousse through before drying, or mist each section with flexible-hold spray before clamping. Heavy oils go on afterwards, never before heat.
2. **Section the hair.** Split the hair into a bottom and top layer, clipping the top up. Work in sections roughly the width of the waver — about 5 to 7 centimetres. Too-thick sections wave unevenly: the outside presses, the inside stays straight.
3. **Clamp and move down.** Starting a few centimetres from the root, clamp the section and hold for around five seconds. Release, then move the waver down so its first barrel sits exactly in the last ridge you just made, and clamp again. This overlap creates the continuous, unbroken wave. Continue to the ends, leaving the last centimetre or two out for a modern finish.
4. **Keep the direction consistent.** The waver only makes waves one way up — do not flip it between sections. Work around the head in the same orientation, front sections included. Flipping the tool breaks the pattern's rhythm and is the most common reason home results look "off" compared with salon photos.
5. **Let everything cool untouched.** Do not brush, shake or finger-comb the waves while they are warm. Pressed waves set as they cool, and disturbing them early is how they drop within the hour. Wait until the hair feels completely cool — ten minutes is a good benchmark.
6. **Finish with gloss, not volume.** Once cool, separate the waves gently with your fingers or a wide-tooth comb — one pass, no more. Smooth a drop of shine serum over the surface, palms flat, following the direction of the waves. Skip volumising and dry texture products; they roughen the surface the style depends on.

On temperature, use a moderate setting — the lowest that still sets your hair. A properly cooled wave at moderate heat outlasts a scorched one.

## 3. The Flat-Iron Alternative

You can approximate mermaid waves with a standard flat iron by rocking it back and forth as you glide down the strand, tilting away from you, then towards you, in alternating bends. It produces a genuine S-wave and needs no extra tool, which makes it the practical option for travel or a one-off occasion.

Be honest about the trade-offs, though: the flat-iron method is slower, because each bend is made individually rather than pressed in one clamp, and the spacing depends entirely on your hand, so the waves are never as uniform. The rhythm tends to drift wider and narrower down the strand, and the result usually lands somewhere between mermaid waves and beach waves.

## 4. The Heatless Braid Method

For a heat-free version, braiding is the classic route. On slightly damp hair, divide the hair into two to four tight, flat plaits, pulled snug and even, and sleep on them overnight. Undo the braids in the morning, shake the waves out gently with your fingers, and finish with a drop of oil. If you prefer a fully heatless routine, this sits in the same family as [sock curls](/hairstyles/waves-curls/sock-curls/) and the [heatless curls overnight](/hairstyles/waves-curls/heatless-curls-overnight/) method — all of them set the pattern while you sleep and ask for patience instead of heat.

The honest caveat: braid waves are softer, narrower and less defined than waver waves. Braids twist the hair slightly as they set it, so the result reads as a gentle crimped wave rather than the flat, glossy S, and the spacing follows your braid tension, which varies. Expect a cousin of mermaid waves, not a copy — and the kindest option for damaged hair. More braids give tighter, more frequent waves; two loose braids give the widest, softest version.

## 5. Variations

### Classic Long Mermaid Waves

The reference version: hair past the chest, waves starting below the cheekbones and repeating all the way down. This is where the triple-barrel method is at its best, because the pattern has room to establish its rhythm.

### Mermaid Waves on Medium Hair

On shoulder to collarbone lengths you get three or four full wave repeats, which is plenty for the style to read clearly. Start the first clamp a little closer to the root than you would on long hair — beginning lower wastes a repeat you cannot spare. Medium hair also holds pressed waves well, since there is less weight pulling them down.

### Mermaid Waves on a Bob

This is the difficult one, and it deserves honesty: short lengths fight the barrel size. A standard triple-barrel waver is wide, and on a chin-length bob you may only fit one full clamp per section — one wave, not a pattern. A mini or slim waver helps, and starting the wave right below the root gives you the best chance of two repeats. The result is a glossy waved bob with a mermaid flavour rather than true mermaid waves — it can look genuinely good, but if the long flowing version is what you are picturing, a bob will not reproduce it.

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/mermaid-waves/mermaid-waves-05.webp" alt="Woman with a chin-length bob styled in glossy pressed waves" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

### Mermaid Waves with a Fringe

A fringe works with mermaid waves as long as it stays out of the waver. Keep a full fringe straight and smooth, and start the waves below it — the contrast between the sleek fringe and the waved lengths is part of the charm. Curtain bangs blend in more easily: wave them lightly, holding the first clamp for less time, so they melt into the front sections. Avoid clamping a short fringe fully; pressed waves on very short hair read as crimping and rarely sit flat.

### Half-Up Mermaid Waves

Wave all the hair first, let it cool completely, then gather the top third into a half ponytail, a small bun or a claw clip. Styling after waving — never before — keeps the pattern unbroken underneath. This variation keeps hair off the face while showing off the pattern, and it is the most forgiving way to wear the style on day two, when the top layer has dropped more than the lengths. Naturally wavy hair can start from [diffuser natural waves](/hairstyles/waves-curls/diffuser-natural-waves/) and blend the half-up section in.

## Mermaid Waves vs Hollywood Waves vs Beach Waves

These three get confused constantly, and they are genuinely different styles. [Hollywood glamour waves](/hairstyles/waves-curls/hollywood-glamour-waves/) are brushed-out curls all turning in a single direction, blended into one smooth, sculpted sheet of S-curves — soft, merged and formal. Mermaid waves keep each pressed ridge separate and crisp, with a regular crimped rhythm that never gets brushed into a sheet. Beach waves are the loosest of the three: irregular, separated, matte-textured bends with straight ends and no fixed pattern.

A quick way to tell them apart in photos: if you can count the individual ridges, it is mermaid waves. If the hair moves as one glossy curtain, it is Hollywood waves. If it looks like it dried that way after a swim, it is beach waves.

## Making Them Last

Pressed waves fail for two reasons: they were disturbed while warm, or the hair was too clean, too heavy or too humid for the set.

- **Product goes in the right order.** Hold products go on before or during styling (mousse on damp hair, flexible spray per section); shine products go on after cooling. Oil before heat weighs the wave down.
- **Respect the cool-down rule.** This is the single biggest factor. A wave that cools completely in shape holds for many hours; a wave brushed warm may not survive one. Short on time, a cool shot from a hairdryer speeds the setting without touching the hair.
- **Sleep on them carefully.** Loosely braid the waved hair or pile it in a high, loose bun before bed; a silk or satin pillowcase reduces the friction that frizzes the surface overnight. In the morning, undo gently, smooth the surface with oiled palms, and re-clamp only dropped front sections.
- **Humidity is the honest limit.** Moisture in the air gradually relaxes a heat-set pattern and lifts frizz at the surface, so on very humid days expect a softer version by evening. An anti-humidity spray helps at the margins; it does not make the style waterproof.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long do mermaid waves last?
On most hair types, mermaid waves made with a triple-barrel waver last a full day looking crisp, and a softer version usually survives into a second day. Letting the waves cool completely before touching them is the biggest factor in how long they hold.

### Can you do mermaid waves without a triple-barrel waver?
Yes, you can create mermaid-style waves without a triple-barrel waver by rocking a flat iron back and forth down each section or by sleeping in tight braids. Both alternatives produce a softer and less uniform result than the waver, which remains the only method that presses the exact, evenly spaced S-pattern.

### Do mermaid waves work on short hair?
Mermaid waves work best on medium to long hair, because the pressed S-pattern needs length to repeat. On a bob, a slim or mini waver can fit one or two waves per section, giving a glossy waved bob rather than the full flowing pattern. Hair shorter than chin length generally cannot complete even one full wave with a standard waver.

### What's the difference between mermaid waves and beach waves?
Mermaid waves are deep, uniform and glossy, with evenly spaced ridges pressed into the hair in a regular rhythm. Beach waves are loose, irregular and matte, with separated bends and straighter ends. You can count the individual ridges in mermaid waves, while beach waves have no fixed pattern.

### How do you make mermaid waves last overnight?
To make mermaid waves last overnight, loosely braid the hair or twist it into a high, loose bun before bed and sleep on a silk or satin pillowcase. In the morning, undo the hair gently, smooth the surface with a drop of oil, and re-clamp only any front sections that have dropped. Avoid brushing, which pulls the pattern out and creates frizz.

### Do mermaid waves damage hair?
Mermaid waves involve heat styling, so they carry the same risk of heat damage as a flat iron or curling iron when used carelessly. Using a heat protectant, choosing the lowest temperature that still sets your hair, and letting sections cool rather than re-clamping them repeatedly all reduce the risk.
