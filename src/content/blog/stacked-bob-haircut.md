Most bobs are judged from the front. The stacked bob is judged from the back. "Stacked" describes how the hair is cut at the nape: short graduated layers are stacked one on top of another, each layer sitting on the one below it, which builds a rounded, full shape at the back of the head. The front is left longer and angled forward, so the silhouette slopes down from a lifted back to longer front points.

If your hair falls flat at the crown and back no matter what you do, this is the bob built to fix exactly that — the volume is cut into the shape rather than styled on top of it. Below are twelve stacked bob styles, an honest explanation of how a stacked bob differs from an A-line or inverted bob, and what the upkeep actually involves.

## 1. What Makes a Bob "Stacked"

The defining feature is graduation. At the nape, the hair is cut very short and close to the head, then each section above it is cut slightly longer, so the layers stack up and outward until they reach the fullest point at the back of the head. From there the cut angles forward, ending in longer pieces at the front. The result is a curved, almost dome-like profile at the back instead of the straight drop of a classic bob.

That back view is the whole point of the cut, which is why a good stacked bob is checked from behind and from the side before it's finished. The graduation has to blend smoothly — visible steps or a shelf where the stack ends mean the blending needs more work. When it's done well, the shape holds itself: the fullness at the back is structural, so the style doesn't collapse by midday the way a flat one-length bob can.

## 2. Classic Stacked Bob

The classic version keeps the stack moderate: a clear rounded shape at the back, sides that angle gently forward, and a length that lands around the jaw at the front. It's the reference point for the style — polished, symmetrical and tidy from every angle.

This is the easiest stacked bob to live with. It suits most hair types with natural straightness or a soft wave and grows out more gracefully than the dramatic versions. If you're trying the cut for the first time, start here.

## 3. Stacked Bob with Side-Swept Fringe

A deep side part and a long, swept fringe soften the precision of the stack. The fringe falls diagonally across the forehead and blends into the longer front section, which breaks up the symmetry and makes the cut feel more relaxed.

This pairing works well if a centre part feels too exposed or your forehead is on the higher side. The swept fringe also gives fine hair at the front something to do — instead of hanging in two flat curtains, it moves as one shaped piece.

## 4. Stacked Bob with Full Fringe

A straight, full fringe with a stacked back is a strong, deliberate look: blunt across the forehead, rounded and full behind. The contrast between the sharp fringe line and the soft graduated back is what makes it striking.

It suits straighter hair best, because the fringe needs to sit flat and even to keep the effect. Be honest about the fringe commitment — full fringes need a quick trim every few weeks to stay above the eyes, on top of the stack's own upkeep.

## 5. Inverted (A-Line-Leaning) Stacked Bob

The inverted stacked bob pushes the angle harder: the back is cut noticeably shorter and higher, and the front drops to chin length or below, creating a steep diagonal line from nape to front points. The stack is still there — this version just leans into the A-line silhouette.

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/stacked-bob-haircut/stacked-bob-haircut-03.webp" alt="Inverted stacked bob with a steep angle, short lifted back and long front points reaching below the chin" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Choose this if you like drama and definition. The steeper the angle, the more the shape depends on the graduation staying crisp, so this version shows regrowth at the nape sooner than the classic.

## 6. Stacked Lob

The stacked lob keeps the graduated back but lets the front grow to collarbone length or just above. The stack is shallower than in a short bob — there's simply more length to distribute — but the back still lifts instead of hanging flat.

This is the version for anyone nervous about going short. You keep length to tie back at the front, the nape stays neat and close, and it grows into an ordinary lob without an awkward stage.

## 7. Stacked Bob for Fine Hair

Fine hair is where the stacked bob earns its reputation. Removing weight at the nape and stacking short layers at the back creates the appearance of thickness and lift that fine hair almost never gets from a one-length cut, which tends to separate and look stringy at the ends.

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/stacked-bob-haircut/stacked-bob-haircut-04.webp" alt="Stacked bob on fine hair showing lifted, full-looking layers at the back and smooth angled sides" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Ask for a moderate stack and minimal texturising — over-thinning fine hair undoes the whole point. For more fine-hair-specific shapes, see our guide to [bob haircuts for fine hair](/hairstyles/bangs-bobs/bob-haircuts-for-fine-hair/).

## 8. Stacked Bob for Thick Hair

Thick hair holds a stack beautifully because there's enough density to build a real rounded shape. The graduation also removes serious bulk from the nape, which makes thick hair feel lighter and cooler, especially in summer.

The watch-out is width: a full stack on very thick hair can puff out at the sides if the graduation is too aggressive. Ask your stylist to soften the silhouette with internal layering so the shape stays curved rather than triangular.

## 9. Curly Stacked Bob

Yes, the stack works on curls — it just has to be cut differently. A curly stacked bob is usually cut dry or with the curl pattern in mind, because curls spring up several centimetres when they dry, and a stack cut wet and straight can end up far shorter and tighter than planned.

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/stacked-bob-haircut/stacked-bob-haircut-05.webp" alt="Curly stacked bob with rounded graduated layers at the back and defined curls framing the face" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Done well, the graduation gives curls a rounded, intentional shape instead of the pyramid silhouette curly bobs often fall into. Keep the stack gentle: curls build their own volume, so they need shaping more than lifting.

## 10. Stacked Bob with Highlights

Colour is a genuine styling tool on this cut. Highlights or lowlights placed through the back sections make the graduation visible — each stacked layer catches the light differently, so the shape reads as deliberate dimension rather than a single block of colour.

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/stacked-bob-haircut/stacked-bob-haircut-06.webp" alt="Stacked bob with highlights through the back sections showing the graduated layers and dimensional colour" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Fine highlights woven through the stack give a subtle effect; chunkier ribbons of colour make the layering graphic and bold. If your hair is one flat colour and your stack looks invisible from behind, this is usually the fix.

## 11. Chin-Length Stacked Bob

The chin-length version is the balanced middle: front points that just graze the chin, a clear stack behind, and nothing long enough to tuck or tie. It frames the jaw neatly and keeps the proportions of the cut obvious.

It's a good choice for petite features, where longer front points can overwhelm, and for anyone who wears glasses — the front sections end right at the frame line rather than fighting with it. The trade-off is zero versatility for updos.

## 12. Stacked Bob with Undercut Nape

Here the very bottom of the nape is clipped short — sometimes to a visible undercut — beneath the stacked layers above it. The stack sits on top like a shelf of fuller hair, and the neckline underneath stays clean and sharp.

This version suits very thick hair (the undercut removes the bulkiest part entirely) and anyone who likes an edge to their cut. It is the highest-maintenance option here: the clipped section shows regrowth within a couple of weeks.

## 13. Textured, Wispy Stacked Bob

The softest take on the style: the stack is there, but the ends are point-cut and feathered so the outline looks wispy rather than sculpted. The back still lifts, but the finish is piecey and undone instead of polished.

Choose this if the classic stacked bob looks too "done" for your taste. It also grows out the most forgivingly of all twelve, because there's no hard line to lose — the shape just gradually softens into a textured bob.

## Stacked vs A-Line vs Inverted vs Classic Bob

These four names get used interchangeably in salons, and they shouldn't be, because they describe different things:

- **Stacked** describes the *layers*: graduated sections stacked at the back to build volume and a rounded shape. A stacked bob can be short or lob-length, steep or gentle.
- **A-line** describes the *length outline only*: shorter at the back, longer at the front, with a clean diagonal line — but no stacked layers. An A-line bob can be cut almost one-length, which means it lies flatter at the back.
- **Inverted** is the loose, everyday word for the same angled outline as an A-line — shorter back, longer front. Many "inverted bobs" also happen to be stacked, which is where the confusion starts, but the word itself refers to the angle, not the layering.
- **Classic bob** is the baseline in our full guide to [bob haircuts](/hairstyles/bangs-bobs/bob-haircuts/): one length all the way around, no stack and no steep angle.

The practical question to ask in the chair is not the name but: "Will the back be graduated and stacked, or one length?" For contrast, the softer European shapes — the [Italian bob](/hairstyles/bangs-bobs/italian-bob/) and the [French bob](/hairstyles/bangs-bobs/french-bob-comeback/) — skip the stack entirely in favour of blunt, bouncy ends, and the [pixie bob](/hairstyles/bangs-bobs/pixie-bob-haircuts/) takes the back shorter still.

## Who It Suits

Fine and flat hair gains the most: if your hair has no natural lift at the crown and back, the stack manufactures it structurally. Straight and softly wavy hair shows the graduation most clearly. Thick hair suits it too, with the softening noted above, and curls work with a curl-aware cut.

The honest cost is upkeep. The nape graduation is a precise shape, and as it grows the stack softens and the clean line goes — typical salon guidance is a trim roughly every four to six weeks to keep the stack crisp, with the inverted and undercut versions at the more frequent end of that range. If you are a twice-a-year haircut person, choose the textured version or a different bob.

## Styling the Stack

The cut does most of the work, but the blow-dry direction decides whether the stack reads as volume or as a bump. Dry the back sections downward and slightly under, following the curve of the head — lifting the back straight up with a dryer fights the graduation and makes it stick out.

A small round brush at the back is the main tool: catch each section, roll it under following the stack's curve, and let it cool before moving on. The front sections just need smoothing in their natural forward direction. Finish by shaking the back gently with your fingers rather than brushing it flat — you want the layers to stay sitting on each other.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What's the difference between a stacked bob and an A-line bob?
A stacked bob has graduated layers cut at the back that stack on top of each other to build volume and a rounded shape. An A-line bob describes only the length outline — shorter at the back, longer at the front — and can be cut almost one-length with no stacking, so it lies flatter at the back.

### Does a stacked bob suit fine hair?
Yes, a stacked bob is one of the best bob shapes for fine hair. The short graduated layers at the back remove weight from the nape and create lift and the appearance of thickness that a one-length bob rarely gives fine hair. Ask for a moderate stack and minimal thinning so the ends stay full.

### How often does a stacked bob need trimming?
A stacked bob typically needs a trim every four to six weeks to keep the graduation crisp. As the nape grows out, the stacked shape softens and the clean back line disappears. Steep inverted versions and undercut napes sit at the more frequent end of that range.

### Can a stacked bob work on curly hair?
Yes, a stacked bob can work on curly hair when it is cut with the curl pattern in mind, ideally dry. The graduation gives curls a rounded shape instead of a triangular one. Keep the stack gentle, because curls create their own volume and mainly need shaping rather than lift.

### Is a stacked bob hard to style?
No, a stacked bob is relatively easy to style because the volume at the back is built into the cut itself. A blow-dry with a small round brush, drying the back sections downward and under along the curve of the head, is usually all it needs. It requires less daily effort than styling volume into a flat one-length bob.

### Does a stacked bob suit a round face?
Yes, a stacked bob can suit a round face well, especially versions with longer angled front sections. The length at the front draws the eye downward and adds vertical lines, while the volume sits at the back rather than at the sides of the face. Avoid very short, wide stacks that add width at cheek level.
