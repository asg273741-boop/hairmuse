A feathered haircut is defined by its finish as much as its cut: layers are sliced so the ends taper into fine points, then styled to sweep back away from the face so each layer lies over the next like the barbs of a feather. It is the cut Farrah Fawcett made famous in the 1970s, and it returns every decade or so — most recently as the quiet, flicky layers all over social media.

What separates feathering from ordinary layering is direction. Layered hair falls where it falls; feathered hair is cut and blow-dried to travel backwards, opening up the face and putting the movement at the sides and ends. The twelve versions below run from the full 70s wing to a feathered pixie. For the broader family first, our [layered haircuts](/hairstyles/haircuts/layered-haircuts/) guide shows where feathering sits among its relatives.

## What Feathering Actually Is

Feathering is a technique applied to the ends of layers. Instead of cutting a layer to one blunt length, the stylist slices into the last few centimetres at an angle, so the ends graduate into a soft V or point — a feathered tip rather than a shelf. Those tapered ends are what allow the hair to curve back and lie over the layer beneath instead of dropping straight.

The second half happens at the mirror, not the chair: feathered hair is blow-dried backwards, brushed away from the face so the tapered ends flick out and settle pointing back. Dried forwards, it looks like an ordinary layered cut; dried backwards, the wings appear. That is why every style below lists styling effort honestly.

## Who Feathered Hair Suits

Feathering flatters oval, heart-shaped and square faces most: the back-swept sides open the face up and the flicked ends soften a strong jaw. Round faces suit it too, provided the shortest layer starts below the cheekbone — wings flipping out at cheek height add width where a round face least wants it.

Two honest caveats. First, this is not a wash-and-go cut: the signature sweep is a blow-dry finish, and air-dried feathered hair reads as ordinary layers with wispy ends. Budget five to ten minutes with a brush and dryer, or choose a softer version (styles 2 and 11) that forgives a rough dry. Second, very fine hair needs the feathering kept light — heavy slicing into fine ends makes them disappear rather than flick (style 8 covers the careful edit).

## 1. Classic Long Feathered Haircut

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/feathered-haircut/feathered-haircut-01.webp" alt="Woman with a classic long feathered haircut with layers sweeping back from the face and flicked feathery ends" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

The reference version: hair worn long, layered from around the cheekbones down, every layer sliced into tapered points and blow-dried back so the sides form soft wings and the ends flick out in graduated rows. It is the direct descendant of the 70s original, minus the hairspray armour — the modern version moves. It suits straight to wavy hair and oval or heart-shaped faces best, and asks the most styling of the twelve: a proper round-brush blow-dry.

## 2. Medium Feathered Layers

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/feathered-haircut/feathered-haircut-02.webp" alt="Woman with a medium feathered haircut at shoulder length with brunette layers sweeping back and flicked ends" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

At shoulder length, feathering is at its most forgiving: the layers start around the jaw, sweep back in two or three visible tiers and flick at the ends, giving the full feathered read without the long version's drying time. It suits most face shapes and survives a rough blow-dry better than any other version — the shorter lengths flick more willingly.

## 3. Short Feathered Cut

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/feathered-haircut/feathered-haircut-03.webp" alt="Woman with a short feathered haircut at jaw length with chestnut layers brushed back and feathery flicked ends" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Cut to jaw length, the feathered shape compresses: longer layers through the top and sides are sliced fine and brushed back, so the outline reads as soft, overlapping feathers rather than a bob's solid edge. It suits round and heart-shaped faces particularly well. Styling takes a few minutes — short feathered hair dries fast and the flicks set with a small round brush — but it needs the most frequent trims of the twelve.

## 4. Feathered Bob

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/feathered-haircut/feathered-haircut-04.webp" alt="Woman with a feathered bob haircut in dark brown with layers sweeping back and ends flicking outward below the chin" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

A bob with its edges feathered instead of blunt: the outline sits just below the chin, but the last few centimetres of every section are tapered and flicked back, so the shape reads polished from a distance and soft up close. It is the office-safe end of the feathered family — the movement is there, the retro signal is not. It suits straight to slightly wavy hair and most face shapes, grows out more gracefully than a blunt bob, and styles with a quick blow-dry flicking the ends back.

## 5. Feathered Haircut with Curtain Bangs

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/feathered-haircut/feathered-haircut-05.webp" alt="Feathered haircut with curtain bangs in honey blonde, the fringe parted in the centre and layers flicking back" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Curtain bangs are feathering's natural fringe: parted in the centre and cut to sweep back into the side layers, they are essentially the first, shortest tier of the feathering itself. The whole front of the hair travels in one direction, which makes this the most cohesive version here — and the most forgiving fringe to grow out, since it simply becomes a shorter layer. It suits oval, long and heart-shaped faces. Our [curtain bangs guide](/hairstyles/bangs-bobs/curtain-bangs-guide/) covers the fringe in detail.

## 6. Feathered Pixie-Length Cut

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/feathered-haircut/feathered-haircut-06.webp" alt="Woman with a feathered pixie-length haircut in copper with longer layers on top brushed back into feathery flicks" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Feathering at pixie length works through the top: the sides and back are kept close and tapered, while the longer top layers are point-cut into fine ends and brushed back so they lie in overlapping, feathery rows. It is softer and more directional than a textured pixie — the movement all travels one way. It suits smaller faces and finer features, and dries fastest of the twelve: a minute with a dryer and a fingertip of wax worked backwards. Trims every four to five weeks keep the top long enough to feather.

## 7. 70s Revival Feathered Haircut

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/feathered-haircut/feathered-haircut-07.webp" alt="Woman with a 70s revival feathered haircut in golden blonde with dramatic feathered wings sweeping back from a centre part" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

The full Farrah: a centre part, long layers cut from the cheekbones, and each side blow-dried into a wide wing that sweeps back and curls under at the ends. Nothing here is subtle, and that is the point — it suits straighter hair that can hold a bend, and oval or long faces. Be honest about the upkeep before booking it: a round-brush or roller blow-dry every wash, fifteen minutes minimum. In exchange, no other version photographs like it.

## 8. Feathered Haircut for Fine Hair

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/feathered-haircut/feathered-haircut-08.webp" alt="Woman with fine ash blonde hair in a feathered haircut at shoulder length with light feathering at the ends sweeping back softly" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Fine hair gets the restrained edit, and the restraint is the whole story: feathering slices ends finer, and on hair that is already fine, aggressive slicing produces transparency, not flicks. The working version keeps the layers long and few, feathers only the last few centimetres, and stays at or above shoulder length so the ends keep their density. Styled back with mousse at the roots, it gives fine hair the one thing it usually lacks — visible movement at the sides. Avoid oils and heavy serums, which collapse the flick.

## 9. Feathered Haircut for Thick Hair

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/feathered-haircut/feathered-haircut-09.webp" alt="Woman with thick dark hair in a long feathered haircut with weight removed and layers sweeping back in soft feathery sections" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Thick hair is feathering's home ground. The slicing that fine hair cannot afford is exactly what thick hair needs: it removes the weight that makes long thick hair sit in one heavy curtain, letting the layers separate into distinct back-swept sections. The result reads lighter without reading thinner, and suits anyone whose hair feels wide and bottom-heavy. Styling effort is moderate, but the flicks hold well once set.

## 10. Curly Feathered Haircut

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/feathered-haircut/feathered-haircut-10.webp" alt="Woman with a curly feathered haircut in dark brown with curls layered and swept back from the face in a soft feathery outline" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

On curls, feathering changes meaning slightly: the tapered ends stop curls stacking into a triangle, and the layers are shaped so the curl pattern sweeps back from the face rather than forward over it. The flick is supplied by the curls themselves, so this is the one version that does not need the backwards blow-dry — diffuse or air-dry and rake the front sections back with cream. It must be cut dry, curl by curl, and suits loose to medium curls best; on very tight curls the taper disappears into the pattern.

## 11. Feathered Lob

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/feathered-haircut/feathered-haircut-11.webp" alt="Woman with a feathered lob haircut at collarbone length with brunette balayage and feathered ends flicking outward" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

The lob — the long bob — with its ends feathered rather than blunt is the most current version in this list: collarbone length, layers kept long and low, just the last tier sliced and flicked back so the outline softens. It keeps the lob's polish while adding movement, and suits almost every face shape. The [lob haircut](/hairstyles/haircuts/lob-haircut/) guide covers the base shape; feathering is the finish you add to it.

## 12. Feathered Haircut with a Money Piece

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/feathered-haircut/feathered-haircut-12.webp" alt="Woman with a feathered haircut with a money piece, long brunette hair with bright blonde face-framing front pieces showing the feathered layers" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Colour placed exactly where the feathering shows: bright face-framing pieces at the front — the [money piece](/hairstyles/hair-color/money-piece-hair/) — run through the shortest, most-swept layers, so the back-swept tiers read as separate ribbons of light and dark. The trade-off is maintenance on two fronts: the flick needs its blow-dry, and the bright front pieces need glossing and gentle treatment, because feathered ends are the oldest, most fragile part of the hair.

## Feathered vs Layered vs Shag

These three get used interchangeably, and they should not be. **Feathering** is a technique plus a finish: layers whose ends are sliced into tapered points, styled to sweep back away from the face. The defining features are the direction (backwards) and the tapered tips — feathered hair is the smoothest of the three, and the only one whose signature look depends on how it is dried.

A **layered cut** is the broader family feathering belongs to: hair cut to two or more lengths, full stop. Layers can fall in any direction and most layered cuts are dried however they land. Every feathered haircut is layered, but most are not feathered — they lack the sliced, back-swept finish. The [layered haircuts](/hairstyles/haircuts/layered-haircuts/) guide maps the whole family.

A **shag** is layering with the opposite temperament: choppy, disconnected layers through the whole head, heavy texture at the crown, and a fringe as the focal point — movement in every direction at once, deliberately undone. Where feathering smooths back, the shag musses up. Our [shag haircut](/hairstyles/haircuts/shag-haircut-for-women/) guide shows that end of the spectrum, and the [octopus haircut](/hairstyles/haircuts/octopus-haircut/) — smooth on top, wispy only at the ends — sits between the two.

The practical rule: choose feathering for polished movement that opens the face, plain layering for shape without a styling commitment, and the shag for texture, crown volume and a fringe-led look.

## How to Style Feathered Hair at Home

The signature move is the backwards blow-dry, and it is worth learning properly because the cut does not do it for you. Work on towel-dried hair with a heat protectant and, unless your hair is fine, a little mousse through the sides. Section by section, place a round brush under the hair and roll it up, back and away from your face as the dryer follows — you are setting each layer to curve backwards and flick at the end. The front sections matter most: roll them back off the forehead and let them cool in that curve before touching them.

Finish by shaking the layers out with your fingers, never a brush, which pulls the flick straight. A light hairspray at arm's length keeps the sweep without the 70s crunch. If your flicks fall within the hour, the culprits are usually hair dried forwards out of habit, or too much heavy product at the ends.

## What to Ask Your Stylist For

Bring photos — feathered means different things in different salons, and in some it still means a full 70s wing. Use the reference points that keep you in control: "layers from the cheekbone" (or jaw, or collarbone — name your shortest layer), "feather the ends, don't blunt them", and "I style it swept back", so the cut is tested in the right direction before you leave. State your effort ceiling honestly: if you will not round-brush, ask for the softer version rather than the full finish you will never do. Fine hair: ends restrained and few. Curly hair: confirm it will be cut dry.

## Maintenance and Grow-Out

Book a trim every six to eight weeks: the tapered ends are the first thing to blur, and once the feathered tips grow out or split, the sweep loses its definition and the cut quietly becomes ordinary layers. Between cuts, condition the ends and protect them from heat — they are the oldest, finest part of each strand. The grow-out is graceful: no harsh line, and a feathered cut degrades into a soft layered cut rather than collapsing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a feathered haircut?

A layered cut whose ends are sliced into fine, tapered points and styled to sweep back away from the face, so the layers lie over each other like the barbs of a feather. Made famous by Farrah Fawcett in the 1970s, feathering is both a cutting technique — the tapered tips — and a styling finish: the backwards blow-dry that makes the wings appear.

### What is the difference between feathered and layered hair?

Layering is the family; feathering is one member of it. A layered cut is hair cut to two or more lengths, in any direction and finish. A feathered cut adds two specifics: the ends are sliced into tapered points rather than cut blunt, and the hair is styled to sweep backwards. Every feathered cut is layered; most layered cuts are not feathered.

### Is a feathered haircut the same as a shag?

No. A shag is choppy, disconnected layering through the whole head with texture at the crown and usually a fringe as the focus — deliberately undone, movement in every direction. Feathering is smoother: tapered ends, one direction (backwards), a polished finish. The full comparison is in the "Feathered vs Layered vs Shag" section above.

### Does a feathered haircut need styling every day?

Honestly, mostly yes — that is the trade at the heart of this cut. The back-swept finish comes from blow-drying the layers backwards with a round brush, five to ten minutes on medium lengths. The softer versions (medium feathered layers, the feathered lob) forgive a rough dry, and a proper blow-dry usually holds two to three days. Air-dried, feathered hair reads as a plain layered cut.

### Can you feather fine hair?

Yes, in its restrained form: few layers, kept long, only the last few centimetres feathered, at or above shoulder length. Slicing heavily into fine ends makes them look sparse instead of flicky. Style with mousse at the roots and keep oils off the ends, which drag the flick down.

### Does feathering work on curly hair?

A version of it. On curls the feathered taper stops the hair stacking into a triangle and shapes the layers to sweep back from the face, while the curls supply the flick themselves — the one version that skips the backwards blow-dry. It must be cut dry, curl by curl, and works best on loose to medium curls.

## Final Thoughts

Feathering rewards honesty about effort: it is the most beautiful finish in the layered family and the least automatic. Decide your length first, then how much blow-drying you will genuinely do, and the twelve styles above narrow quickly — full wings at one end, the barely-there feathered lob at the other. And if the comparison section left you wanting texture in every direction instead of one polished sweep, the shag is waiting at the louder end of the same family.
