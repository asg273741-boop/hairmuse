The milkmaid braid is the updo that looks like a weaving project and is actually two ordinary plaits. The hair is parted down the middle, braided into two low pigtail plaits, then each plait is lifted up, laid across the top of the head like a headband and pinned on the opposite side. That is the whole construction — no braiding around the head, no working blind behind your own ears, nothing to unpick and restart. It is the easiest route to the full crown effect, it keeps every strand off your face and neck, and a well-pinned one survives a working day, a wedding and most of a night's sleep.

One clarification before the steps, because the two styles are confused constantly: a milkmaid braid is **not** a crown braid. A milkmaid is two finished plaits crossed over the head and pinned. A crown braid is one continuous braid woven around the head as it is made, feeding in new hair the whole way round. Same halo silhouette from the front, completely different build — and the milkmaid is far more forgiving. If you want the full family of woven versions, our [crown braid hairstyles guide](/hairstyles/braids/crown-braid-hairstyles/) covers twelve of them, with the milkmaid included as the easy option; this page is the deep dive on the milkmaid itself, with the full tutorial and its own variations. The plaiting itself is a plain three-strand braid throughout, but if your plaits tend to come out loose or lopsided, our [beginner braid guide](/hairstyles/braids/beginner-braid-guide/) fixes the basics first.

## 1. The Classic Milkmaid Braid

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/milkmaid-braid/milkmaid-braid-01.webp" alt="Woman with chestnut brown hair in a classic milkmaid braid with two plaits crossed over the top of her head" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

The reference version, and the one the step-by-step below teaches: two smooth, even plaits crossed over the crown so their ends meet and tuck behind the opposite plait, forming one unbroken braided band from ear to ear. Nothing hangs down, the parting stays visible as a clean centre line at the back, and the band sits where a headband would — a couple of centimetres behind the front hairline, which is the most flattering position on most faces. **Difficulty:** easy — the only genuinely new skill is pinning so the pins disappear. **Length:** armpit length or longer for the full cross-over; see the length section below for the honest maths.

## 2. Step 1: Part Down the Middle and Prep

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/milkmaid-braid/milkmaid-braid-02.webp" alt="Woman parting her long brown hair down the middle into two equal sections ready for milkmaid braids" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Start with dry, brushed, tangle-free hair. Second-day hair grips best; freshly washed hair is slippery, so work a little dry shampoo or texture spray through it first if it has just been washed. Using a tail comb, draw a straight centre parting from the forehead all the way down to the nape — check the back in a second mirror, because a wandering back parting is the one flaw this style cannot hide. Gather each side into a loose, low bunch in front of the shoulder while you work on the other. Everything in this style is symmetrical, and the symmetry is won or lost here, before a single plait exists.

## 3. Step 2: Braid Two Low Plaits

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/milkmaid-braid/milkmaid-braid-03.webp" alt="Woman braiding one of two low three-strand plaits over her shoulder for a milkmaid braid" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Plait each side into an ordinary three-strand braid, starting just below the ear and working down in front of the shoulder where you can see what you are doing. Keep the tension firm and even — a slack plait stretches longer when lifted but looks untidy across the crown, while an even one lies flat and wide. Braid as far down as you possibly can, to within a couple of centimetres of the ends: every centimetre of plait is reach you will need at the cross-over. Secure each with a small, clear elastic. If your hair is layered and the ends poke out, braid a fraction tighter and mist the last section with water; the damp ends bind together instead of escaping.

## 4. Step 3: Lift and Cross the First Plait

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/milkmaid-braid/milkmaid-braid-04.webp" alt="Woman lifting one finished plait up and across the top of her head to form a milkmaid braid" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Take the right plait and lift it up and over the top of your head, laying it across the crown so its end comes to rest just behind your left ear. Position the band a few centimetres behind the hairline rather than on it — plaits laid directly on the hairline slip forwards and look like a hat brim. Hold the plait in place with one hand and, with the other, push two bobby pins through its end, crossing them in an X and aiming them against the direction of the plait so they lock into the hair beneath. The tail and its elastic should sit tucked behind the ear, hidden by the plait itself and the hair at the side of the head.

## 5. Step 4: Cross the Second Plait and Tuck the Ends

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/milkmaid-braid/milkmaid-braid-05.webp" alt="Woman crossing the second plait over her head and pinning it to complete a milkmaid braid" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Now lift the left plait up and over in the opposite direction, laying it alongside — or fractionally behind — the first, so the two form one continuous band with no gap at the top of the head. Its end tucks behind the right ear, exactly where the first plait began, and gets the same crossed pair of pins. This is the moment the style becomes a circle: each plait's tail hides behind the other plait's starting point, which is why the finished band looks endless. Add one or two extra pins along each plait wherever it lifts away from the head, pushing them in underneath the braid so only the plait shows.

## 6. Step 5: Pancake, Soften and Set

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/milkmaid-braid/milkmaid-braid-06.webp" alt="Woman gently pulling out the loops of her finished milkmaid braid to widen and soften it" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

With both plaits pinned, go along each one and tug the outer loops gently outward — pancaking — working a few millimetres at a time so the band widens evenly and any thin spots fill in. This step is what turns two schoolgirl plaits into the full, romantic band in the photographs, and it also disguises slightly uneven tension. Pull a couple of fine strands loose at the temples if you want the softer, modern finish, smooth flyaways down with your palms, and finish with a light mist of hairspray held at arm's length. The whole build, once practised, takes five to ten minutes.

## 7. Double Milkmaid Braid

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/milkmaid-braid/milkmaid-braid-07.webp" alt="Woman with dark brown hair wearing a double milkmaid braid with two rows of plaits crossed over her head" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Instead of two plaits, make four: split each side in two, plait all four thinly, and cross them over in pairs so two parallel braided rows circle the crown. The effect is a wider, more intricate band — closer to a woven coronet — and it is the best milkmaid for very thick hair, which makes a single pair of plaits too bulky to lie flat. Cross and pin the front pair first, then the pair behind it. **Difficulty:** easy to moderate; it is the classic build with twice the pinning. **Length:** armpit length or longer — four thinner plaits still each need to cross the head.

## 8. Milkmaid Braid with a Ribbon

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/milkmaid-braid/milkmaid-braid-08.webp" alt="Woman with blonde hair in a milkmaid braid with a deep red ribbon woven through the plaits" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Tie a slim ribbon — velvet for winter, gingham or satin for summer — around the base of each plait before you start braiding, and treat it as part of one strand, so it spirals through the plait as you work. Cross the plaits over as usual and let the ribbon ends finish in small bows tucked behind the ears, or trim them flush and pin them under. The ribbon does practical work as well as decorative: it gives slippery, freshly washed hair something to grip, and the colour shows the braid's path across the crown beautifully. **Difficulty:** easy. **Length:** the same cross-over reach as the classic.

## 9. Messy Boho Milkmaid Braid

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/milkmaid-braid/milkmaid-braid-09.webp" alt="Woman with wavy light brown hair in a loose messy boho milkmaid braid with strands framing her face" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

The undone version: start from waved or slept-in hair rather than smooth lengths, plait loosely, cross over, then pancake aggressively — pulling loops well out so the band looks twice its width — and free plenty of strands around the face and nape. Nothing should line up too neatly; an elastic left slightly visible is a fault in the classic and a shrug here. This is the festival and holiday version, and the most forgiving of all, because the deliberate mess hides every uneven stitch. It pairs naturally with the looser styles in our [boho braids collection](/hairstyles/braids/boho-braids/). **Difficulty:** easy. **Length:** shoulder length can just manage this one if the plaits are loose and pinned low — see below.

## 10. Half Milkmaid for Shorter Hair

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/milkmaid-braid/milkmaid-braid-10.webp" alt="Woman with shoulder-length hair wearing a half milkmaid braid with two small plaits pinned across the front of her head and the rest loose" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

The honest short-hair answer. Instead of plaiting all the hair, take a generous section from above each ear, plait just those, and cross them over the front of the crown, pinning the ends behind the opposite ear under the loose hair — the rest stays down. You get the milkmaid's braided headband line across the front, which is the part everyone sees, without needing the length for a full circuit. It works from a long bob upwards and is the version to reach for while growing out a shorter cut. **Difficulty:** easy. **Length:** chin to shoulder length and up.

## Milkmaid Braid vs Crown Braid: Which One Are You Picturing?

Most photographs labelled either name show the same halo of braid, so judge by construction, not by the picture. If the style was made by plaiting two pigtails and pinning them over, it is a milkmaid — this page. If it was woven in one continuous circuit around the head, adding hair as it went, it is a crown braid. The milkmaid wins on speed, on being able to see everything you are doing, and on suiting a first attempt. The crown braid wins on seamlessness — there are no pinned joins at all — and on hold, because a woven braid locks into itself. Many people learn the milkmaid first, wear it for a season, and graduate to the full crown once the halo shape feels familiar; our [crown braid guide](/hairstyles/braids/crown-braid-hairstyles/) has the woven step-by-step when you are ready.

## A Note on Length: The Honest Version

A full milkmaid braid needs each plait to travel from below one ear, up and across the top of the head, and still have an end left to pin behind the other ear. In practice that means **armpit-length hair or longer** for the classic cross-over; bra-strap length is comfortable, and waist length gives you tails to spare. Shoulder-length hair is the borderline: the plaits reach across but the ends land on top of the head rather than tucking behind the ear, so they must be pinned flat and pancaked over — the messy boho version above disguises this best. Below shoulder length, a full milkmaid is not a pinning problem to solve but a geometry problem that cannot be solved; wear the half milkmaid (style 10) instead, which needs only enough length for two short plaits across the front. Extensions or a braided hairpiece pinned over the crown are the remaining option for a special occasion, and there is no shame in them — stylists use them constantly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a milkmaid braid the same as a crown braid?

No. A milkmaid braid is two ordinary plaits, braided hanging down, then lifted, crossed over the top of the head and pinned. A crown braid is a single braid woven continuously around the head as it is made. They look similar from the front, but the milkmaid is much easier and needs no braiding behind your own head.

### How long does hair need to be for a milkmaid braid?

Armpit length or longer for the classic version, because each plait must cross the whole head and still tuck behind the opposite ear. Shoulder-length hair can manage a low, loose, boho version with the ends pinned on top. Shorter than that, the half milkmaid — two small plaits across the front, the rest loose — is the workable option.

### How long does a milkmaid braid take to do?

Five to ten minutes once you have done it twice. The plaits themselves take a minute or two each; most of the time goes on positioning the cross-over and pinning invisibly. The first attempt usually takes fifteen minutes, largely spent re-pinning the ends.

### How do I stop the pins and elastics showing?

Tuck each plait's end behind the opposite ear, underneath the other plait's starting point, and pin with two bobby pins crossed in an X, pushed in under the braid against its direction. Use clear or hair-coloured elastics, braid as close to the ends as you can, and pancake the loops over the join — the widened braid covers its own fixings.

### Can a milkmaid braid be slept in?

Yes — it is one of the style's real advantages. Pinned snugly, it usually survives the night and looks softer rather than ruined in the morning. Sleep on a smooth pillowcase, and in the morning smooth the surface with damp palms and re-pin any loop that has worked loose. Two days is the realistic limit before it needs redoing.

### Does a milkmaid braid work on fine or layered hair?

It does, with small adjustments. Fine hair makes slimmer plaits, so pancake generously and add texture spray first for grip and apparent width; the double milkmaid also disguises thinness well. With layers, braid slightly tighter, mist the ends with water so short pieces bind in, and expect a few wisps to escape — the boho version turns that into the look rather than a flaw.

## Final Thoughts

The milkmaid braid is the rare impressive updo that is exactly as simple as it looks in a diagram: two plaits, crossed over, pinned down. Learn the pinning — crossed pins, ends hidden behind the ears — and the style is yours for anything from a Tuesday to a wedding.
