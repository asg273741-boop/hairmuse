The octopus haircut is named for its silhouette: a smooth, full top that sits like the rounded head of an octopus, with the lengths below thinned into separated, wispy strands that fall like tentacles. It comes from Korean and Japanese salons, and its trick is disconnection — the top layer keeps its length and weight while the layers underneath are cut shorter and finer, so the ends separate into distinct pieces instead of one solid curtain of hair.

That contrast is exactly what makes it more forgiving than it looks. The top stays sleek and substantial, so the cut reads polished from the front, while the wispy ends keep long hair from dragging. The twelve versions below run from the classic long cut to short, curly and fine-hair edits. For the wider family first, our [layered haircuts](/hairstyles/haircuts/layered-haircuts/) guide shows where the octopus cut sits among its relatives.

---

## Who the Octopus Cut Suits

The cut flatters oval, long and heart-shaped faces most, because the full top adds width at the crown while the fine ends stop long hair overwhelming the face. It suits straight to wavy hair of medium density best — that is the texture it was designed on. Two honest caveats: on very fine hair the tentacle ends can look sparse rather than wispy, so the layering has to stay restrained (style 7); and on very curly hair the disconnection blurs, because curls separate on their own — the curly version (style 9) works, but it reads as a softer, rounded layered cut rather than a true octopus shape.

## 1. Classic Long Octopus Cut

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/octopus-haircut/octopus-haircut-01.webp" alt="Woman with a classic long octopus haircut with a smooth full top and wispy tentacle-like layered ends" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

The reference version: hair worn long, the top layer smooth and full to around the chest, then the underneath layers cut progressively shorter and finer so the last twenty centimetres separate into wispy, tentacle-like strands. The disconnection is visible but the outline stays sleek, which is why it suits straight to slightly wavy hair so well. Styling effort is low — the separation is cut in, and a smoothing blow-dry through the top is all it asks for.

## 2. Medium Octopus Cut

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/octopus-haircut/octopus-haircut-02.webp" alt="Woman with a medium octopus haircut with a smooth rounded top and wispy layered ends past the shoulders" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

At just-past-shoulder length, the octopus shape is at its most wearable: the rounded top layer keeps its fullness, and the wispy ends start around the collarbone, so the tentacle effect shows without needing very long hair. It is long enough to tie back and light enough to wear loose daily. It suits most face shapes and straight to wavy hair, and the grow-out into the long version is seamless.

## 3. Short Octopus Cut

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/octopus-haircut/octopus-haircut-03.webp" alt="Woman with a short octopus haircut at chin length with a voluminous top and tapered wispy strands below the jaw" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Cut to chin or jaw length, the octopus becomes a rounded top with fine strands tapering below the jaw — the shortest the shape can go before the tentacles disappear entirely. It keeps the jawline light and works well on round and heart-shaped faces. Styling effort is a few minutes with a dryer to keep the top smooth, and it needs the most frequent trims of the twelve to hold its proportions.

## 4. Octopus Cut with Curtain Bangs

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/octopus-haircut/octopus-haircut-04.webp" alt="Octopus haircut with curtain bangs parted in the centre and a smooth top falling into wispy layered ends" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Curtain bangs are the natural fringe pairing: parted in the centre, sweeping to the cheekbones and blending into the top layer, they soften the forehead while the lengths below do the wispy work. They suit oval, long and heart-shaped faces, and they grow out into face-framing layers without an awkward stage. Styling is thirty seconds with a round brush to set the sweep.

## 5. Octopus Cut with a See-Through Fringe

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/octopus-haircut/octopus-haircut-05.webp" alt="Octopus haircut with a light see-through wispy fringe, smooth top and fine layered ends" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

The see-through fringe — feathered and light enough to see the forehead through — is the classic Korean partner to this cut, and it echoes the wispy ends so the whole style feels of a piece. It flatters smaller faces and finer foreheads where a full fringe would dominate. The trade-off is upkeep: a trim every three to four weeks and a quick pass with the dryer to stop it separating.

## 6. Octopus Cut for Thick Hair

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/octopus-haircut/octopus-haircut-06.webp" alt="Octopus haircut on thick hair with a smooth full top and weight removed so the ends separate into wispy strands" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Thick hair is where the disconnection earns its keep: the underneath layers take out the bulk that makes long thick hair flare into a triangle, while the top layer stays long and smooth, so the hair falls in separated strands instead of one heavy mass. It suits anyone whose long hair feels wide and bottom-heavy. Styling effort is moderate — thick hair needs its blow-dry, but the cut removes the daily battle with volume at the ends.

## 7. Octopus Cut for Fine Hair

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/octopus-haircut/octopus-haircut-07.webp" alt="Octopus haircut on fine hair at shoulder length with a fuller top and only the ends lightly layered" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Fine hair gets the careful edit, and honesty matters here: cut full-strength, the tentacle ends can read as thin and stringy rather than wispy. The fix is restraint — keep the top layer long and near one-length for density, disconnect only the last few centimetres, and stay at or above shoulder length. Done that way, the ends suggest movement without spending fullness. Style with mousse, not oils, which drag fine ends straight down.

## 8. Wavy Octopus Cut

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/octopus-haircut/octopus-haircut-08.webp" alt="Wavy octopus haircut with a smooth crown and loose waves breaking into wispy layered ends" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

On loose waves, the octopus layering gives the wave pattern somewhere to go: the crown stays smooth and full, and the waves break into separated, piecey ends instead of one wide mass. The layers should be cut a little longer than on straight hair, because waves spring up as they dry. Styling effort is low — scrunch in a light cream, air-dry or diffuse, and the cut supplies the shape.

## 9. Curly Octopus Cut

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/octopus-haircut/octopus-haircut-09.webp" alt="Curly octopus haircut with a fuller crown and curls separating into layered strands through the lengths" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

On curls, expect a relative of the octopus rather than the exact shape: curls separate naturally, so the tentacle effect is built in, and the cut's job becomes keeping the crown smooth and the volume distributed so the silhouette stays rounded instead of triangular. It must be cut dry, curl by curl. It suits loose to medium curls best; on very tight curls the disconnection disappears entirely and you simply have a good layered curly cut.

## 10. Octopus Cut with Face-Framing Layers

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/octopus-haircut/octopus-haircut-10.webp" alt="Long octopus haircut with shorter face-framing pieces cascading from the cheekbones and wispy ends at the back" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Here shorter pieces cascade from the cheekbones down the front while the back keeps the classic smooth-top, wispy-end shape. The diagonal front lines draw the eye to the face and soften a strong jaw, which makes this the most flattering version for round and square faces. Styling effort is low-to-moderate: the front pieces want their curve set with a brush or roller, while the back can dry as it likes.

## 11. Ash Blonde Octopus Cut

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/octopus-haircut/octopus-haircut-11.webp" alt="Ash blonde octopus haircut with a sleek smooth top and fine wispy layered ends" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Colour changes how this cut reads: on cool ash blonde, the separated ends catch the light individually, so the tentacle effect is the most visible of any version — which also means it is the least forgiving of dry, over-bleached ends. The wispy layering must be cut into healthy hair, and bond-building care is part of the deal. It suits straight to wavy hair and cooler skin tones, and it is the version most likely to be stopped and asked about.

## 12. Grown-Out Octopus Cut

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/octopus-haircut/octopus-haircut-12.webp" alt="Grown-out octopus haircut with very long hair and softer, less defined wispy layered ends" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Four to six months on, the octopus softens: the top layer lengthens, the ends stay separated but lose their deliberate edge, and the whole thing reads as a light, piecey layered cut. Many people arrive here by skipping appointments rather than booking it, which is fine — but know the trade-off: the signature silhouette is a fresh-cut effect, and once the shortest underneath layer passes the shoulders, the tentacles effectively retire until the next reshaping.

## Octopus Cut vs Wolf Cut vs Hush Cut

These three are confused constantly, and the differences are real. An **octopus cut** disconnects: the top layer stays long, smooth and full, while the layers underneath are cut shorter and finer, so only the ends separate into wispy, tentacle-like strands. The contrast lives at the bottom of the hair, and the overall read is sleek.

A **wolf cut** puts the contrast at the top instead: short, choppy layers stacked through the crown, shaggy texture all over, and longer lengths at the back with a mullet-adjacent edge. Where the octopus is smooth on top and wispy below, the wolf cut is wild on top and wispy below. Our [wolf cut for women](/hairstyles/haircuts/wolf-cut-for-women/) guide shows that end of the spectrum.

A **hush cut** removes the contrast altogether: soft layers blended through the whole head on one gradual gradient, ends feathered, silhouette curving inward — no disconnection anywhere. It is the quietest and most polished of the three; the [hush cut](/hairstyles/haircuts/hush-cut/) guide covers it in full. The [shag haircut](/hairstyles/haircuts/shag-haircut-for-women/) sits outside this trio — feathered and textured through every layer, flickier than all of them.

The practical rule: choose the octopus cut for a sleek top with movement only at the ends, the wolf cut for crown volume and attitude, and the hush cut for softness with no visible layering at all.

## How to Style an Octopus Cut at Home

The finish to aim for is smooth on top, separated at the ends. Blow-dry the top layer downward with a paddle brush or the concentrator nozzle, keeping the surface sleek; then work a pea-sized amount of light serum or texture spray through the last third of the lengths and twist small sections with your fingers so the ends fall as distinct strands rather than a sheet. A straightener passed quickly through the ends, bending each section very slightly outward, sharpens the tentacle effect. Keep heavy waxes and oils off the top layer — weight there collapses the rounded shape the cut depends on.

## What to Ask Your Stylist For

Bring photos, and use the reference points that keep this cut in octopus territory: "keep the top layer long and smooth", "disconnect the layers underneath", "wispy, separated ends — not blended layers". Specify the length the top layer must keep (chest for the classic, collarbone for the medium), and where the disconnection should start. If your hair is fine, say the ends must stay restrained and few; if it is thick, ask for the weight to come out underneath, not off the top. If your hair is curly, confirm it will be cut dry.

## Maintenance and Grow-Out

Book a trim every six to eight weeks to keep the disconnection deliberate: the wispy ends are the first thing to blur, and once the underneath layers catch up with the top layer, the silhouette quietly becomes an ordinary layered cut. The honest upside is that nothing about the grow-out looks wrong — there is no harsh line to outgrow, the fringe options merge into face-framing, and the cut degrades gracefully into style 12 rather than collapsing. Budget-wise it sits in the middle of the layered family: more upkeep than a hush cut, less than a blunt bob.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is an octopus haircut?

A layered cut from Korean and Japanese salons named for its silhouette: a smooth, full top layer like an octopus's head, with the underneath layers disconnected and thinned so the ends separate into wispy, tentacle-like strands. The contrast sits at the ends — the top stays sleek and substantial, unlike shaggier layered cuts.

### What is the difference between an octopus cut and a wolf cut?

Where the contrast lives. An octopus cut keeps the top long and smooth and disconnects only the underneath layers, so just the ends separate. A wolf cut stacks short, choppy layers at the crown with texture all over and a mullet-adjacent back. Octopus reads sleek; wolf reads wild. The full three-way comparison, including the hush cut, is in the section above.

### Does an octopus cut work on fine hair?

Yes, but only in its restrained form: the top layer kept long and near one-length, the disconnection limited to the last few centimetres, and the overall length at or above the shoulders. Cut full-strength, fine hair's wispy ends look sparse and stringy instead of piecey. Style with mousse rather than oils to keep the ends light.

### Can you get an octopus cut with curly hair?

A version of it. Curls separate naturally, so the tentacle effect is built in, and the cut focuses on a smooth crown and evenly distributed volume. It must be cut dry, curl by curl. On loose to medium curls it reads clearly; on very tight curls the disconnection disappears and the result is simply a well-shaped layered curly cut.

### How do you style an octopus cut at home?

Blow-dry the top layer smooth and downward, then work a little light serum or texture spray through the ends and twist small sections so they fall as separate strands. A quick pass with a straightener, bending each end section slightly outward, sharpens the effect. Keep heavy products off the top layer so the rounded shape holds.

### How often does an octopus cut need trimming?

Every six to eight weeks to keep the ends deliberately separated — the disconnection is a fresh-cut effect and blurs as the underneath layers grow. The grow-out itself is graceful: no harsh lines, fringes merge into face-framing layers, and the cut softens into a light layered style (style 12) rather than losing its shape all at once.

## Final Thoughts

The octopus cut is a study in contrast placed carefully: all the sleekness on top, all the movement at the ends. Decide your length first, then how much disconnection your hair type can honestly carry, and the twelve styles above narrow quickly. And if the comparison section left you wanting the texture everywhere rather than just at the ends, that is useful information too — the wolf cut and the shag are waiting at the louder end of the same family.
