The low bun is the hardest-working updo there is. It sits at the nape, works with a centre or side parting, survives under a hat or a bike helmet better than any high style, and moves from school run to wedding guest without changing its basic mechanics. What changes is the finish: smooth it down and it is a chignon, rough it up and it is a messy bun, braid it first and it holds all day on fine hair that defeats every other style.

This is the gallery version of the low bun: twelve styles built on one foundation. Learn the five-step basic bun first — every style below is that same bun with one variable changed. Two relatives deserve their own deep dives: the fully sleek, gel-smooth version is taught step by step in our [slick back bun tutorial](/hairstyles/updos-buns/slick-back-bun/), and the art of making a loose bun actually stay put is covered in our guide to [messy buns that hold](/hairstyles/updos-buns/messy-buns-that-hold/).

## The Basic Low Bun in 5 Steps

1. **Gather low.** Brush or rake the hair back to the nape, deciding your parting first — the parting is set before the ponytail, not after. The ponytail should sit where the skull curves into the neck, not on the neck itself.
2. **Secure the base.** Fasten with a proper elastic, not a stretched-out one. This base is the whole foundation; every bun below inherits its hold from this step.
3. **Twist the tail.** Twist the ponytail in one direction until it starts to coil against itself, then let it wrap around the base.
4. **Pin at four points.** Push bobby pins in at roughly 12, 3, 6 and 9 o'clock, wavy side down, aiming into the elastic. Four well-placed pins beat ten hopeful ones.
5. **Finish honestly.** Either smooth the surface with a brush and a whisper of spray for a polished bun, or pull a few pieces loose for a soft one. Pick one — a bun that tries to be both just looks unfinished.

## 1. Classic Low Chignon

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/low-bun-hairstyles/low-bun-hairstyles-01.webp" alt="Woman with chestnut brown hair in a classic smooth low chignon bun at the nape of her neck" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

The reference low bun: hair smoothed back, coiled flat against the nape, not a strand out of place. The chignon differs from the basic bun in one respect — the tail is wrapped in a flat spiral rather than a round coil, which makes the silhouette wider and lower, more oval than ball. Tuck the ends underneath and pin from below so no hardware shows. A centre parting makes it formal; a deep side parting makes it vintage. This is the sleek end of the low-bun spectrum, and if the glassy, slicked finish is what you are after, our [slick back bun tutorial](/hairstyles/updos-buns/slick-back-bun/) teaches that technique in full — gel layering, the two-stage ponytail, flyaway control. **Best for:** weddings, interviews, evening wear. **Length:** shoulder length and below.

## 2. Low Messy Bun

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/low-bun-hairstyles/low-bun-hairstyles-02.webp" alt="Woman with honey blonde hair in a relaxed low messy bun with soft loose strands escaping" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

The chignon's relaxed sister. Build the basic bun but skip the smoothing: gather with your fingers rather than a brush, twist loosely, and pin only where the coil actually wants to fall apart. Then pull gently at the loops to widen them and tease a few strands free around the hairline. The trick is that the mess is added *after* the structure, never instead of it — a bun with no foundation is not messy, it is just falling down. If yours reliably collapses by lunchtime, the foundation techniques in our [messy buns that hold](/hairstyles/updos-buns/messy-buns-that-hold/) guide — the cross-pin anchor and the U-pin through the centre — fix exactly that, and they work at the nape just as well as at the crown. **Best for:** everyday wear, second-day hair. **Length:** chin length and below.

## 3. Braided Low Bun

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/low-bun-hairstyles/low-bun-hairstyles-03.webp" alt="Woman with dark brown hair in a low bun with a side braid wrapping into it" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Braid first, then coil. A simple three-strand plait down the ponytail, wrapped around its own base, gives you a bun with two advantages: the braid's texture reads as intricate from across the room, and the interlaced strands hold far better than a plain twist — this is the low bun to choose for a long day, a windy day, or fine slippery hair. Gently pull the edges of the plait wider before coiling (pancaking) if you want more volume from less hair. A side braid swept into the bun, as pictured, is the prettiest variation; a fishtail version is slower but finer-textured. **Best for:** long days, fine hair that escapes plain buns. **Length:** shoulder length minimum, better below.

## 4. Twisted Low Bun

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/low-bun-hairstyles/low-bun-hairstyles-04.webp" alt="Woman with auburn hair in a twisted low bun made from two rope twists coiled at the nape" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Split the ponytail in two, twist each half in the same direction, then twist the two ropes around each other in the opposite direction — the counter-twist is what stops the rope unravelling. Coil the rope around the base and pin. The result has a woven, almost knotted texture that looks considerably more complicated than the four minutes it takes. Because the hair is locked inside the rope, this is another strong choice for layered hair: short pieces are trapped in the twist instead of poking out of a coil one by one. Keep the ropes loose for a soft finish or twist them firmly for a sleeker, more sculptural bun. **Best for:** layered hair, textured finishes. **Length:** shoulder length and below.

## 5. Low Bun with Face-Framing Pieces

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/low-bun-hairstyles/low-bun-hairstyles-05.webp" alt="Woman with dark blonde hair in a low bun with soft face-framing pieces left loose around her face" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

The difference between a bun that looks severe and one that looks romantic is usually two strands of hair. Before gathering, section off a slim piece on each side — from the temple, about the width of your little finger — and leave them out of the ponytail entirely. Build any low bun behind them, then give the loose pieces a soft bend away from the face with a tong or a straightener flick. Curtain fringes and grown-out bangs do this job automatically; just leave them out when you gather. Keep the pieces fine: chunky sections read as hair you forgot, while slim ones read as a decision. **Best for:** softening any bun, date nights, photos. **Length:** any length that reaches a bun, as long as the front pieces are long enough to bend.

## 6. Low Space Buns

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/low-bun-hairstyles/low-bun-hairstyles-06.webp" alt="Woman with black hair in two small low space buns, one at each side of the nape" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Space buns dropped to the nape. Part the hair straight down the middle, make two low ponytails behind each ear, twist and coil each one, and pin. The low placement changes the whole personality of the style — where high space buns are playful and festival-bright, the low pair reads softer and more deliberate, closer to two small knots than to mouse ears. It is also genuinely practical: the parting splits the hair's weight in half, so each bun is light, secure, and comfortable, and the style works on hair too short for one decent-sized bun. Twist the ends out slightly for a spikier finish, or keep the coils smooth and tucked for a neater one. **Best for:** short-to-medium hair, festivals, fun. **Length:** chin length and below.

## 7. Low Bun for Weddings

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/low-bun-hairstyles/low-bun-hairstyles-07.webp" alt="Woman with light brown hair in an elegant bridal low bun decorated with delicate pearl pins" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

The bridal low bun is the basic bun with every detail upgraded: volume built at the crown before gathering (a little backcombing or a root-lift spray), the coil pinned invisibly, and the surface finished strand by strand rather than sprayed into submission. It is chosen for weddings for practical reasons as much as pretty ones — it sits below where a veil comb grips, it does not fight with a high neckline or statement earrings, and it photographs cleanly from every angle, all day. Pearl pins, a small comb or fresh flowers pushed into the coil are the classic decorations; add them last, into the finished bun, never as part of its structure. **Best for:** brides, bridesmaids, wedding guests. **Length:** shoulder length and below; padding can fake the rest.

## 8. Low Bun with a Scarf or Claw Clip

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/low-bun-hairstyles/low-bun-hairstyles-08.webp" alt="Woman with wavy medium brown hair in a casual low bun with a silk scarf tied around it" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Two ways to let an accessory do the decorating. The scarf version: build a plain low bun, then tie a silk scarf around its base and knot it underneath, leaving the tails to hang — the bun becomes the backdrop and the scarf becomes the style, which makes this the fastest way to make a third-day bun look intentional. The claw clip version skips the coil altogether: twist the ponytail upward against the nape, fold the ends down, and clamp the whole twist with a large claw clip. It takes twenty seconds, holds surprisingly well on medium hair, and comes out without a single pin mark. **Best for:** bad hair days, holidays, minimal effort. **Length:** scarf — shoulder and below; claw clip — chin length and below.

## 9. Low Ballet Bun

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/low-bun-hairstyles/low-bun-hairstyles-09.webp" alt="Woman with sleek dark hair in a neat tight low ballet bun at the nape" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

The dancer's bun, worn at the nape instead of the crown. Everything here is about security and smoothness: hair brushed thoroughly back, the ponytail elastic doubled, the coil wrapped tightly and flat, pinned at four points, and — the dancer's real secret — a fine hairnet over the finished bun, twisted and pinned so it vanishes while holding every wisp in place. A little gel or wax worked through before gathering keeps the surface clean. The result is the most formal, longest-wearing low bun in this list; it will outlast a performance, a flight, or a full day in uniform. The low placement makes it kinder for long wear than the traditional crown position. **Best for:** dance, uniformed work, maximum hold. **Length:** shoulder length and below.

## 10. Low Bun for Fine Hair (The Donut Trick)

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/low-bun-hairstyles/low-bun-hairstyles-10.webp" alt="Woman with fine blonde hair in a full-looking low bun made with a hair donut" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Fine hair makes a small bun — unless something else provides the bulk. That something is a hair donut: make the low ponytail, pull it through a foam donut matched to your hair colour, then spread the hair evenly over the ring so the foam disappears, tuck the ends underneath and pin. The bun that results is two or three times the size your own hair could make, perfectly round, and light enough to wear all day. Two refinements matter: choose a smaller donut than you think (fine hair spreads thin over a big one and the foam peeks through), and rough up the ponytail with texture spray first so the hair grips the foam instead of sliding off it. **Best for:** fine or thin hair wanting a substantial bun. **Length:** chin length and below.

## 11. Low Bun for Thick Hair

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/low-bun-hairstyles/low-bun-hairstyles-11.webp" alt="Woman with very thick dark brown hair in a large secure low bun at the nape" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Thick hair has the opposite problem: plenty of bun, not enough hold. A single elastic asked to secure both the ponytail and the coil will lose by midday, so split the job — one strong elastic for the base, then coil in two halves, pinning each half separately with long U-pins that can actually carry the weight. Spin pins (the corkscrew kind) are the other thick-hair tool worth owning: two twisted in will hold what eight bobby pins cannot. Expect a bigger, more generous silhouette than the other buns here and lean into it; fighting thick hair into a small neat coil is a battle the hair usually wins. **Best for:** thick, heavy, or very long hair. **Length:** shoulder length and below, the longer the better.

## 12. The 2-Minute Everyday Low Bun

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/low-bun-hairstyles/low-bun-hairstyles-12.webp" alt="Woman with medium brown hair in a quick simple everyday low bun at the nape" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

The bun you will actually wear most: gather, and on the last pull through the elastic, stop halfway so the hair forms a loop with the ends hanging. Wrap the ends around the base to hide the elastic, tuck them in, and pull the loop a little wider. No pins, no mirror required, genuinely two minutes — and because the loop is not a tight coil, it does not leave a crease when you take it down. It is less secure than the pinned versions and will need a redo after exercise or a nap on the sofa, but for the school run, the desk, and the dog walk, it is the right amount of hairstyle. **Best for:** daily wear, working from home, hair-down-later days. **Length:** shoulder length and below.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do you do a basic low bun?

Gather the hair into a low ponytail at the nape, twist the tail until it coils around the base, and pin at four points — roughly 12, 3, 6 and 9 o'clock — pushing the pins into the elastic. Smooth the surface for a polished finish or loosen a few pieces for a soft one. The five-step version at the top of this article is the full method.

### Do low buns work on short hair?

On chin-length hair and below, yes — with adjustments. A single bun will be small, so use the donut version for size, the double low space buns to split the hair into two manageable halves, or pin shorter layers up in sections. Hair above the chin generally cannot reach a nape bun without pieces falling short at the back.

### How do you make a low bun stay in all day?

Build the hold at the base: a firm elastic, a twist or braid that locks the hair (styles 3 and 4), and pins that reach the elastic rather than skimming the surface. Second-day hair or texture spray gives slippery hair grip. For maximum security, the ballet bun's hairnet trick holds every wisp without extra product.

### Are low buns damaging to hair?

They are among the gentlest updos, because the weight hangs from the nape rather than pulling at the hairline and crown. Keep them healthy by using snag-free elastics, not winding the same tight coil in the identical spot every day, and taking the bun down rather than sleeping in a tight one regularly.

### What is the difference between a chignon and a low bun?

A chignon is a specific low bun: the hair is coiled or rolled flat against the nape in a smooth, wide spiral, traditionally with the ends tucked invisibly. Low bun is the umbrella term — messy, braided, twisted and loop buns all sit at the nape too, but only the smooth flat coil earns the chignon name.

### Can you do a low bun on fine hair?

Yes — fine hair just needs the bulk supplied from elsewhere. The donut version (style 10) is made for it, and the braided and twisted versions help because interlaced hair looks and holds thicker than a plain coil. Texture spray before you start makes a visible difference to how full the finished bun looks.

## Final Thoughts

Twelve styles, one foundation. Once the five-step basic bun is in your hands, the only question each morning is the finish — smooth, messy, braided, accessorised. Start with the version that solves your hair's actual problem (donut for fine, split-and-U-pin for thick, braid for slippery) and the low bun stops being a style you attempt and becomes the one you rely on.
