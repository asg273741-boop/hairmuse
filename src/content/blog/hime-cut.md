A hime cut is a traditional Japanese princess cut built from three precise elements: blunt sidelocks cut level with the cheeks or jaw — called hime-soku — a straight-across fringe, and long, straight lengths left to fall loose down the back. The contrast is the whole point: short, graphic pieces framing the face, and uninterrupted length behind them. It dates back to the Heian era, when women of the imperial court wore it, and the silhouette has barely changed since.

It is trending again now because anime heroines and J-pop and K-pop idols have put it in front of a new audience. The twelve styles below run from the faithful classic to softened, modern versions, with honest notes on who each suits and what it takes to maintain.
## 1. What Defines a Hime Cut

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/hime-cut/hime-cut-01.webp" alt="Woman with a classic hime cut with straight black hair, blunt cheek-length sidelocks, a straight-across fringe and long straight lengths" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Three elements must all be present. First, the hime-soku: sidelocks in front of the ears, cut blunt to a single level line, usually at the cheekbone or jaw. Second, a straight-across fringe at or just above the brows. Third, long lengths worn straight and loose, with no layers breaking up the back.

That is what separates a hime cut from ordinary long hair with bangs, which has one continuous shape; a hime cut has three deliberate shapes that do not blend. If the sidelocks melt into face-framing layers, or the fringe is wispy and parted, you have a hime-inspired cut — pretty, but a different thing.
## 2. Classic Long Hime

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/hime-cut/hime-cut-02.webp" alt="Classic long hime cut on waist-length straight dark hair with blunt jaw-level sidelocks and a full straight fringe" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

The reference version: sidelocks cut level at the jaw, a full straight fringe, and lengths falling to the waist or beyond, worn glass-straight. Everything is blunt and symmetrical, and the effect is formal in the best way — closer to a ceremony hairstyle than a casual one.

This version asks the most of your hair: lengths need to be healthy and dense enough to hold a clean line, and naturally straight hair does most of the work. Bring it as your reference photo — every other style here is a variation on its proportions.

## 3. Hime with Blunt Micro Fringe

Here the classic silhouette stays, but the fringe shrinks to a [micro bangs](/hairstyles/bangs-bobs/micro-bangs/) length — an inch or two above the brows. The shorter fringe sharpens the whole cut and throws the focus onto the eyes and the level line of the sidelocks.

It is also the least forgiving fringe here: it shows regrowth quickly, sits badly on a strong cowlick, and cannot be pinned away. Choose it only if short fringes already suit you, and budget for trims every couple of weeks.

## 4. Hime Bob

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/hime-cut/hime-cut-03.webp" alt="Hime bob haircut with chin-length blunt lengths, cheek-level sidelocks and a straight-across fringe on straight brown hair" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

The short version keeps all three elements but pulls the lengths up to the chin or just below, so sidelocks and back almost meet — the most wearable, least costume-like way into the cut.

It is also the easiest hime to grow out of. Keep the ends blunt rather than textured — the clean outline is what keeps it reading as a hime cut rather than an ordinary bob with bangs.

## 5. Layered Soft Hime

In the soft version, the sidelocks are lightly point-cut instead of left dead blunt, and a few gentle layers run through the ends of the lengths. The three elements are still visible, but the feathered edges move more and read less severe.

This is the version most Western salons will suggest first, and it suits anyone who likes the shape but works in a conservative setting. The trade-off is definition: soften it too far and it becomes a layered cut with bangs, so keep the sidelock line recognisable.

## 6. Hime with Curtain Bangs

Swapping the straight fringe for curtain bangs changes the mood completely. Parted in the centre and sweeping out to the cheekbones, they blend into the sidelocks so the face frame flows instead of stopping at a hard line — our [curtain bangs guide](/hairstyles/bangs-bobs/curtain-bangs-guide/) covers lengths and styling in detail.

Strictly speaking this is a hime-inspired hybrid rather than a true hime cut, since the straight fringe is a defining element. But it is the version fringe-shy readers will actually wear, and it grows out gracefully, with no blunt fringe line to maintain.

## 7. Wavy Hime

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/hime-cut/hime-cut-04.webp" alt="Wavy hime cut with loose waves through the long lengths, blunt sidelocks and a straight fringe kept smooth" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

A wavy hime lets loose waves run through the long lengths while the sidelocks and fringe are kept smooth and straight. The mix of textures is striking — the graphic face frame stays, and the back softens into something more romantic.
Be honest about the upkeep first: naturally wavy hair means straightening the sidelocks and fringe every day while encouraging wave everywhere else — two routines on one head. It works best on hair straight at the front and wavy through the lengths, or on straight hair waved with a tong.

## 8. Hime with Wispy Fringe

A wispy, see-through fringe lightens the front of the cut: feathered ends, a little skin showing through, and the sidelocks left blunt so the signature line survives. It is the gentlest fringe option here, and the kindest to fine hair, which can look sparse in a thick blunt fringe.

It is also low-commitment — it softens into face-framing pieces within weeks if you change your mind. Ask for the centre kept slightly longer than the edges so it does not separate into two curtains by midday.

## 9. Chin-Length Sidelocks

This variant changes the proportions rather than the elements: the sidelocks are cut longer, level with the chin instead of the cheekbone, so the step down to the lengths is smaller and the whole cut reads calmer.

It is a good adjustment for round or full faces, where cheek-level sidelocks land at the widest point and emphasise it; chin-level sidelocks draw the eye downward instead. In reverse, cheek-level sidelocks add width where a long, narrow face wants it.

## 10. Hime with Undercut Sidelocks

Here the sidelocks are undercut or shaved underneath, so the blunt hime-soku line sits over a much thinner underlayer. With the hair down it looks like a classic hime; the shaved section shows only when the sidelocks are tucked or clipped back.

The practical benefit is comfort: thick hair loses the hot, heavy panel in front of the ears, and the sidelocks lie flatter. The cost is upkeep — the undercut needs re-clippering every few weeks, and growing it out means a stretch of uneven sidelocks.

## 11. Two-Tone Hime

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/hime-cut/hime-cut-05.webp" alt="Two-tone hime cut with platinum blonde sidelocks and fringe over long straight black lengths" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Colour can underline the cut's geometry. Dyeing the sidelocks and fringe in a contrasting shade — platinum against black, copper against chocolate, a pastel over natural brown — makes the three elements unmistakable and turns the face frame into the whole statement.
Subtler versions keep one colour family and lighten the face-framing pieces a few shades. Either way, be realistic: a precision cut plus a two-part colour means salon visits for both, and coloured sidelocks show regrowth against the blunt line quickly.

## 12. Hime for Fine Hair

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/hime-cut/hime-cut-06.webp" alt="Hime cut on fine straight blonde hair with blunt mid-length lengths, neat sidelocks and a light straight fringe" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Fine hair can wear a hime cut, but the proportions need protecting. Keep the lengths at mid-back or above rather than waist-length, so the ends stay full, and keep every edge blunt — feathering removes density fine hair cannot spare.

The fringe is the decision point: a full blunt fringe can look thin on fine hair, so the wispy version (style 8) or a slightly narrower fringe often works better. The sidelocks are a gift, though — a clean level line at the cheek makes fine hair look deliberately styled rather than limp.

## 13. Hime with C-Curl Ends

In this modern Korean-influenced version, the ends of the lengths — and sometimes the tips of the sidelocks — are styled into a soft inward C-curve instead of left dead straight. The three elements stay intact, but the finish is polished and bouncy rather than severe.

It is a styling choice more than a different cut, so it sits on top of the classic, bob or soft versions: a large-barrel tong or a round-brush blow-dry creates the curve in minutes, and on naturally straight hair it drops out by evening — an easy way to test a softer mood without changing the cut.

## Hime Cut vs Jellyfish, Hush and Octopus Cuts

All four cuts come out of the same Japanese and Korean salon scene, but they are built differently. A [jellyfish haircut](/hairstyles/haircuts/jellyfish-haircut/) stacks two tiers — a rounded bob-like top over long lengths — with a visible step all around the head. A hime cut has no top tier: its short pieces exist only at the front, and the back stays one unbroken length.

A [hush cut](/hairstyles/haircuts/hush-cut/) is the soft opposite: wispy layers and a light fringe blended through the whole head, with no blunt lines anywhere. An [octopus cut](/hairstyles/haircuts/octopus-haircut/) keeps length too, but heavily layered on top with thinner, tentacle-like ends. For clean geometry and a formal finish, choose hime; the other three trade its precision for blend and movement.

## Who It Suits — and Who Should Skip It

Naturally straight hair types, roughly 1a to 2a, are the ideal base: the sidelocks hang level, the fringe sits flat, and the lengths fall straight without daily heat. Thick straight hair holds the blunt lines beautifully, and fine straight hair works in the protected proportions of style 12.

Be honest if your hair is wavy or curly: on type 2b hair or above, a true hime cut means straightening the sidelocks and fringe daily — the moment they curl, the level lines disappear — and most people tire of it within a month; a soft, hime-inspired version is wiser. On face shapes: oval and heart faces take the classic proportions easily; round faces should lengthen the sidelocks to chin level; long faces can use cheek-level sidelocks and a full fringe to add width. Skip it if you hate salon maintenance or wear your hair up most days, since the sidelocks will not reach a ponytail.

## What to Ask Your Stylist

Bring a photo of the exact version you want, ideally showing the sidelocks in profile, because sidelock length defines the cut. Name the reference point out loud — cheekbone, jaw or chin — rather than saying "short at the front," which can be read as ordinary face-framing layers.

Then specify the fringe: full and straight across, at or just above the brows, and wide enough to meet the sidelocks without a gap. Ask for the sidelocks cut blunt and level, in front of the ears, and the back lengths left one length with no layers. If you want a softened version, say which element may be softened — fringe, sidelocks or ends — so the others stay precise.

## Styling and Upkeep

Day to day, styling is simple: dry the fringe and sidelocks downward with a paddle brush so they lie flat and level, smooth the lengths with a brush or a quick pass of a straightener, and finish with a drop of light oil on the ends. The sidelocks are the tell — if they flip or separate, the cut looks untidy, so style them first.

The upkeep is the honest catch: precision shows regrowth fast, so expect fringe and sidelock trims every few weeks, with a full tidy of the lengths every couple of months. Many salons will do a quick fringe-and-sidelock trim between appointments. If that rhythm sounds like too much, the soft or curtain-bang versions forgive a few extra weeks.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does a hime cut suit round faces?

Yes, with one adjustment: have the sidelocks cut at chin length rather than cheekbone length. Cheek-level sidelocks land at the widest point of a round face and can emphasise it, while chin-level sidelocks draw the eye downward and make the face look longer. A full straight fringe also helps by shortening the face visually.

### How often does a hime cut need trimming?

The fringe and sidelocks need trimming every few weeks, because even a small amount of regrowth makes their level lines look uneven. The long lengths need a tidy far less often, roughly every couple of months. It is a precision cut, so it shows regrowth faster than a layered cut does.

### Can you get a hime cut with curly hair?

You can, but it will not stay a hime cut on its own. The sidelocks and fringe must sit straight and level for the cut to read correctly, so curly hair means straightening those sections every day. Most stylists will suggest a soft, hime-inspired version instead, which keeps the face-framing idea without the daily heat styling.

### What's the difference between a hime cut and a jellyfish cut?

A hime cut keeps its short pieces only at the front — blunt sidelocks and a straight fringe — while the back stays one long, unbroken length. A jellyfish cut has a rounded, bob-like top layer that goes all the way around the head, with long lengths falling from underneath it. In profile, the jellyfish shows a step at the back; the hime cut does not.

### How do you style the sidelocks?

Blow-dry the sidelocks downward with a paddle brush straight after washing, before they dry in a flipped position. A quick pass with a straightener on a low-to-medium heat setting levels them if they have dried unevenly. They are short, so this takes a minute or two, but it is the step that makes the whole cut look deliberate.

### Does a hime cut work on short hair?

Yes — the hime bob keeps all three elements, with the lengths cut to chin length or just below. The sidelocks sit close to the length at the back, so the effect is subtler than the classic long version. It is the easiest hime style to maintain and the simplest to grow out if you change your mind.
