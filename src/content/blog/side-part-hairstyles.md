A side part is the cheapest haircut you will ever get: no scissors, no colour, just a comb and thirty seconds. Moving your part off-centre changes the whole architecture of a style — it adds height on one side, sweeps hair diagonally across the forehead, and breaks the symmetry that can make a familiar cut feel flat. This guide is written for women's hair specifically (the search results mix in men's cuts, which play by different rules), and it covers the part itself as much as the styles: how to find the part your hair actually wants, how to move it, and what to expect while it adjusts.

Start with the honest bit. Your hair has a natural part — the line where it falls most easily, usually set by your growth pattern and cowlicks. You can absolutely override it, but a trained part does not relocate overnight: expect three to seven days of the hair arguing back, flipping over by lunchtime, before the roots learn the new direction. Retrain it by parting damp hair, blow-drying the roots flat in the new direction (heat sets the root angle), and sleeping with it parted the new way. Once it settles, the payoff is real — hair parted against its natural fall lifts at the root, which is why a fresh side part often looks like a volume treatment.

Two more principles run through all 12 styles below. First, depth is a dial: a subtle part, just off centre, reads polished and modern; a deep part, aligned with the highest point of the eyebrow or beyond, reads dramatic and creates the most volume. Second, the heavy side does the work — the side with more hair gets the height and the sweep, so build volume there with a root-lifting product and a round brush, and keep the light side sleek and tucked.

## 1. Deep Side Part Bob

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/side-part-hairstyles/side-part-hairstyles-01.webp" alt="Deep side part bob with a glossy sweeping front" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

The signature version: a chin- or jaw-length bob parted deeply, roughly in line with the outer edge of the eyebrow, so the front section sweeps across the forehead in one long diagonal. The heavy side gets a soft bend and visible root lift; the light side tucks cleanly behind the ear. It suits almost every face shape because the diagonal interrupts both width and length at once, and it is the fastest way to make a blunt bob look styled rather than simply cut. Ask for the part to be considered at the cutting stage — a bob cut on a centre part and worn on the side can sit unevenly at the front corners.

## 2. Side-Swept Pixie

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/side-part-hairstyles/side-part-hairstyles-02.webp" alt="Pixie cut with a long side-swept top section" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Short hair takes a side part better than any other length, because the longer top section has somewhere to go. Keep the top four to six inches long, part it deeply, and sweep the whole section across and slightly forward; the shorter side and back keep the shape crisp. The sweep softens the pixie's edges, adds a fringe effect without a fringe trim schedule, and gives fine hair the illusion of density — hair lying over hair reads thicker than hair standing alone. Style with a pea-sized amount of wax worked through dry hair, pushing the top in the direction of the sweep.

## 3. Long Layers with a Side Part

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/side-part-hairstyles/side-part-hairstyles-03.webp" alt="Long layered hair worn with a soft side part" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

On long hair, a side part stops layers from hanging in two equal curtains. The heavy side falls in a long diagonal across one cheekbone, which shows off the layering where it is most visible, while the light side can tuck behind the ear to open up the face. This is the most forgiving entry point if you are part-sceptical: nothing about the cut changes, the effect is entirely in the styling, and you can flip back to centre tomorrow. Blow-dry the front section of the heavy side away from the face with a round brush to get the swoop that makes it look deliberate.

## 4. Side Part Lob

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/side-part-hairstyles/side-part-hairstyles-04.webp" alt="Shoulder-length lob styled with a side part and loose waves" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

The lob and the side part were made for each other: shoulder length has enough weight to hold a sweep but enough movement to keep it soft. Part it just beyond the centre for an everyday version, or deep for the evening one, and add loose waves concentrated on the heavy side. Because the lob's outline is blunt, the asymmetry of the part is what supplies the shape — without it, a grown-out lob can read triangular. If your lob is due a refresh, our [lob styling ideas](/hairstyles/haircuts/medium-length-layered-haircuts/) cover the mid-length shapes that pair best with an off-centre part.

## 5. Curly Side Part

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/side-part-hairstyles/side-part-hairstyles-05.webp" alt="Curly hair with a side part and volume on the heavy side" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Curls plus a side part equals volume with a postcode: nearly all of it lives on the heavy side, which is exactly where you want it. Part curly hair when wet, using the end of a comb rather than dragging it through dry curls, and let the heavy side dry piled high — clipping the roots up while drying exaggerates the lift. Do not fight for a ruler-straight line; a slightly soft, finger-placed part looks more natural in texture. Watch the balance over the day: if the light side starts to look flat by comparison, a small amount of volume powder at its root evens things out.

## 6. Side Part with Curtain Bangs

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/side-part-hairstyles/side-part-hairstyles-06.webp" alt="Side part combined with curtain bangs sweeping open" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Curtain bangs are usually a centre-part style, but shifting the part to the side turns them into a long, sweeping fringe that opens diagonally — softer than a blunt fringe, more structured than plain layers. One side of the curtain becomes the long sweep; the other tucks shorter and blends into the front layers. It is a good compromise for anyone growing out a fringe, because the side part disguises uneven lengths. Our [curtain bangs guide](/hairstyles/bangs-bobs/curtain-bangs-guide/) covers the cut itself; the styling change here is simply to blow-dry the fringe across the forehead rather than splitting it down the middle.

## 7. Sleek Side Part

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/side-part-hairstyles/side-part-hairstyles-07.webp" alt="Sleek straight hair with a sharp side part" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

The polished extreme: hair smoothed completely flat, parted with a tail comb into a line sharp enough to read across a room, usually worn tucked behind both ears with the ends glassy. It works on any length from bob to waist and is the side part's formal register — the one that pairs with tailoring and evening wear. Precision is the whole style, so the details matter: part on freshly washed hair, smooth a serum through the lengths before straightening, and set the part line itself with a fingertip of gel or a fine mist of hairspray on a toothbrush to catch flyaways along the part.

## 8. Side Part Ponytail and Updo Crossover

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/side-part-hairstyles/side-part-hairstyles-08.webp" alt="Low ponytail styled from a side part" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Everything in this guide still applies when the hair goes up — in fact, a side part is what separates an interesting updo from a scraped-back one. Part first, then gather: a low ponytail or bun built on a side part keeps the diagonal sweep across the forehead and a little height at the crown, while the light side pulls back cleanly. Leave the front section of the heavy side loose until last and pin it into the sweep, rather than dragging it straight back. It takes an extra twenty seconds and makes gym hair, wedding hair and office hair all look intentional.

## 9. Side Part for Round Faces

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/side-part-hairstyles/side-part-hairstyles-09.webp" alt="Side part hairstyle balanced for a round face shape" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

If there is one face shape the side part was invented for, it is round. A centre part mirrors a round face's symmetry and can emphasise width at the cheeks; a deep side part breaks the circle with a diagonal, adds height at the crown on the heavy side, and draws the eye upward rather than across. Go deep rather than subtle, keep the light side tucked or slim so it does not add width, and favour lengths at or below the chin — a sweep that lands at the jaw extends the diagonal furthest. The same asymmetry strategy runs through our [layered haircuts for round faces guide](/hairstyles/haircuts/layered-haircuts-for-round-faces/).

## 10. Side Part for Fine Hair: The Volume Trick

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/side-part-hairstyles/side-part-hairstyles-10.webp" alt="Fine hair with a side part flipped for root volume" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Here is the trick stylists use on fine hair, and it is almost cheating: part your hair on the opposite side to the one it wants. Hair that has lain in one direction for months lies flat out of habit; flipped against its trained direction, the roots stand up instead of lying down, and the heavy side gains visible lift with no product at all. It works best combined with the retraining routine from the introduction — a few days of blow-drying the roots in the new direction — after which the volume becomes the default rather than the exception. Keep products light: a root spray on the heavy side only, never an oil, which fine hair cannot afford near the part.

## 11. Wet-Look Side Part

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/side-part-hairstyles/side-part-hairstyles-11.webp" alt="Wet-look hair sculpted into a deep side part" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

The editorial version: hair combed into a deep side part and coated to a glossy, just-emerged-from-water finish, with the heavy side sculpted into a smooth wave over the forehead. Work a generous amount of gel or a gel-and-oil mix through damp hair, comb the part and the sweep into place, then do not touch it while it sets — every touch breaks the surface. It is a one-evening style rather than an everyday one, but for events it reads more deliberate than almost anything else on this list, and it photographs exceptionally well because the part line stays graphic.

## 12. The 90s Side Part Revival

<figure class="article-image"><img src="/images/articles/side-part-hairstyles/side-part-hairstyles-12.webp" alt="90s-inspired side part with a flipped front section" width="896" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

The decade's signature part is back: deep, a little exaggerated, with the front section flipped over so the heavy side falls in a smooth curtain and the ends kick out slightly. On 90s cuts — chunky layers, lobs, long bobs — it is the finishing detail that makes the revival read authentic rather than accidental. The modern update is softness: the original was lacquered stiff, while today's version keeps the shape but lets it move, set with a flexible spray rather than a hard one. If you are trying it on a layered cut, the flip works best when the shortest front layer starts around the cheekbone.

## Which Side Should You Part On?

There is no universally correct side, but there are three useful tests:

- **The natural fall test** — after washing, shake your head and let the hair settle untouched. Where it divides on its own is your natural part. Parting there is easiest and sleekest; parting against it gives the most volume.
- **The stronger-side test** — most faces are slightly asymmetrical, and most people have a side they prefer in photographs. Parting so the heavy side sweeps towards your preferred side puts the volume and the diagonal on the side you show the camera.
- **The feature test** — a sweep draws the eye along its diagonal. Parting on the left sends hair — and attention — across to the right, and vice versa. If you want to play down one side of the face, let the light, tucked side be that one.

Face shape refines the depth more than the side: round and square faces flatter with a deeper part and a longer diagonal; long faces suit a subtler part, since a deep one adds height they do not need; oval faces can wear any depth. When in doubt, start subtle for a week, then go one step deeper — the dial only turns one way once you see the volume.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I find my natural part?

Wash your hair, comb it all straight back from the hairline while it is still damp, then push it gently forward with your palms and let it fall. It will divide along its natural part — the line set by your growth pattern. That is the part that takes the least effort to keep and lies the flattest. Any other part is a trained part, which is completely achievable; it just needs a few days of retraining.

### How long does it take to retrain a part?

Usually three to seven days. Part the hair damp in its new place, blow-dry the roots flat in the new direction (heat is what resets the root angle), and wear it parted the new way as much as possible, including overnight. The hair will flip back at first, especially around any cowlick; by the end of the first week it should hold through the day with only a touch of root product.

### Is a side part better than a centre part?

Neither is better in general — they do different jobs. A centre part is symmetrical, polished and elongating, and suits oval and long faces particularly well. A side part is asymmetrical, adds root volume on the heavy side, and softens width, which is why it flatters round and square faces. Many people keep both: centre for sleek styles, side for volume days. Your hair's natural fall also votes — fighting it daily costs more effort than either choice is worth.

### Which side should I part my hair on?

Use the tests in the section above: your natural fall for ease, the opposite side for maximum root volume, or the side that sweeps hair towards the side of your face you prefer. There is an old salon rule about parting on the left for a more classic look and the right for a softer one, but in practice the growth pattern and your own asymmetry matter far more than the compass point.

### Does a side part make hair look thicker?

It can, especially on fine hair. Parting against the trained direction lifts the roots, and the heavy side stacks hair over hair, which reads as density. A deep part also concentrates more hair on one side, so that side looks fuller. The effect is styling, not new growth — but combined with blunt ends and light root product rather than oils, it is one of the most reliable volume illusions available.

### Can I wear a side part with bangs?

Yes — the side part changes what the bangs do rather than ruling them out. Curtain bangs parted to the side become a long diagonal sweep; a full fringe can be parted slightly off-centre and swept so it sits asymmetrically; and a side part with a short fringe worn straight still reads clean, with the asymmetry happening above it. The one combination to approach carefully is a deep part with a blunt fringe, which can look disconnected unless the fringe is swept too.
